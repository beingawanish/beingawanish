Chapter 13: Food Delivery

Type every file in order. Run each checkpoint before moving on. Checkpoints run from the code/ folder (the one holding food_delivery/). The full suite runs from the guide root (the folder holding code/ and tests/). Python 3.13, stdlib only.

Food delivery looks like ride-sharing with a kitchen bolted on. That's half right. You still match a moving partner to a point on a map, and you still chase an offer that nobody answers. But now there are three parties instead of two, money flows back out as refunds, and the bill has more lines than the menu. It has become one of the most-asked LLD problems of the last few years. Interviewers like it because it touches nearly every move in this guide at once: a cart with a rule, a pricing pipeline, a state machine with refunds, a dispatch loop with timeouts, retries that must not double-charge, and notifications going everywhere.

1. The problem

Build the order core of a Swiggy- or DoorDash-style app. Restaurants publish menus, and each item can be switched on or off. Restaurants have opening hours and can pause orders when the kitchen is slammed. A customer fills a cart, and a cart holds food from one restaurant only. At checkout the bill is worked out step by step: item total, delivery fee by distance, coupons and automatic offers, then tax. Placing an order must be safe to retry.

The restaurant accepts or rejects. Once it accepts, a delivery partner is offered the job. If they decline or ignore it for 30 seconds, the next partner gets it. The order moves through PLACED, ACCEPTED, PREPARING, READY, PICKED_UP and DELIVERED, or ends early as REJECTED or CANCELLED with a refund that depends on who cancelled and how far the food got. The customer sees an ETA and gets a notification at every step.

2. Requirements

Clarifying questions to ask first (with the answers this chapter assumes)

  • One restaurant per cart, or many? One. Adding an item from a second restaurant is an error unless the customer says "replace my cart." This is what the real apps do, and it keeps one order equal to one kitchen and one pickup.
  • Are prices locked when the item goes into the cart? No. The cart stores item ids and quantities. Prices and availability are read from the live menu at checkout, so a sold-out item is caught before payment, not after.
  • What goes on the bill, and in what order? Item total, then a delivery fee of a base plus a per-km rate, then discounts, then tax on food after discounts. Order matters: tax after the coupon is a business decision, and the design must make it visible.
  • How do coupons work? One coupon code per order. Codes can have a minimum spend and a cap. Some offers need no code, like free delivery above a threshold.
  • When is a partner assigned? When the restaurant accepts, not when the customer pays. The partner rides over while the kitchen cooks.
  • What if a partner doesn't answer? The offer expires after a timeout and moves to the next-nearest partner. After three attempts the platform gives up, cancels, and refunds in full.
  • Who can cancel, and what do they get back? The customer: full refund before cooking starts, half while cooking, nothing allowed once the food is ready. The restaurant or the platform: always a full refund, because it isn't the customer's fault.
  • What does "safe to retry" mean? The client sends an idempotency key with every place-order call. The same key returns the same order and never charges twice. The same key with a different request is a client bug and gets an error.
  • Is this one process? Yes, for the interview. Section 9 covers what changes with many servers.

Functional

  • Restaurants with a location, opening hours [opens_at, closes_at), a prep time, a pause switch, and a menu of items that can be marked unavailable.
  • A per-customer cart with the single-restaurant rule, a per-line quantity cap, removal, and an explicit replace=True to switch restaurants.
  • A quote and a checkout that re-validate the cart against the live menu and opening hours.
  • A pricing pipeline: item total → delivery fee → discount chain → tax → Bill. Coupons: percent-off with a cap, flat-off, both with a minimum spend. Automatic offer: free delivery above a threshold.
  • Idempotent order placement keyed by (customer, idempotency key).
  • The order state machine above, with every change recorded in a history with time and actor.
  • Partner assignment through a pluggable strategy (nearest free partner within a radius by default), with decline, timeout, reassignment, and give-up-and-cancel after a maximum number of attempts.
  • An ETA at placement, recomputed when a partner accepts, behind an estimator interface.
  • Refunds computed by a policy from who cancelled and the current status.
  • Notifications to any number of observers. A failing observer never breaks the order.

Non-functional

  • Time is injectable. Opening hours, offer timeouts and ETAs all read the clock, so tests freeze it.
  • Money is Decimal, rounded half-up to cents at each step.
  • Thread-safe: two retries of the same request create one order, and one partner is never offered two orders at once.
  • Every number lives in a frozen, validated config dataclass.
  • Typed exception hierarchy; no bare except. Stdlib logging; print only in the demo.
  • Stdlib only.

The goalposts the interviewer will move. In a 2026 round the first forty minutes get you to a working order flow. The last fifteen are follow-ups like these, each answered in section 9: "now let a customer order from two restaurants," "now batch two orders onto one rider," "now run three app servers," "now add surge delivery fees when it rains," "now support scheduled orders for tomorrow lunch." The design should take each one as a new class or a contained edit, not surgery.

5. Key design decisions

  1. The state machine is a table, not a tangle of ifs. TRANSITIONS maps each status to the statuses it may move to. Order.advance() checks the table and appends a StatusChange. Nothing else changes a status. Adding a status like ON_HOLD is one new row and a few new entries, and the tests can assert on the table itself.
  2. Who did it matters as much as what happened. Every change records an Actor. The refund policy reads it: a customer cancelling while the food cooks gets half back, and the restaurant cancelling at the same moment refunds in full. Same transition, different money. Without the actor you can't tell those apart.
  3. The cart stores intent; checkout reads the truth. The cart keeps item ids and quantities only. _checkout_lines reads prices and availability from the live menu under the lock. A price change or a sold-out item between "add" and "pay" is caught at the one moment it matters.
  4. Pricing is a pipeline, and discounts are a chain. The pipeline is a list of steps that share one PricingContext. The list order is the rule "tax after discounts." Discounts are a Chain of Responsibility: each DiscountRule sees the context and decides whether it applies. A new offer is a new rule linked into the chain. The pipeline doesn't change.
  5. Dispatch is its own object with its own lock. The Dispatcher owns the life of an offer: claim a partner, wait, release on decline or timeout, try the next, give up after N attempts. The AssignmentStrategy only answers "which partner?" Splitting these lets you swap nearest-first for rating-weighted without touching the timeout logic.
  6. Claiming a partner is compare-and-set. PartnerRepository.reserve() marks a partner busy only if they're free, and says whether this call won. That one atomic step is why two orders can't both grab the same rider, even if the strategy picks the same person for both.
  7. Timeouts are pulled, not pushed. The service has a tick() that a scheduler calls every few seconds. It asks the dispatcher for stale offers. No background threads hide inside the domain, and tests drive time by advancing a FixedClock and calling tick().
  8. Idempotency lives at the front door. place_order checks the (customer, key) index before it looks at the cart, because a retry arrives after the first call has already emptied the cart. A matching key with a matching request returns the stored order. A matching key with a different drop location or coupon raises IdempotencyKeyReusedError.
  9. Events are published after the lock is released. Each use case collects its events while holding the lock and publishes them after. A slow SMS gateway then can't stall every other order in the system.

Patterns, and why each earns its place. State machine as data, because the lifecycle is the thing interviewers poke hardest. Pipeline plus Chain of Responsibility for pricing, because bills grow new lines every quarter. Strategy for assignment, refunds and ETA: three policies that product teams change on their own schedule. Observer for notifications: the order flow shouldn't know who is listening. Repository for storage, with the idempotency index and the partner claim as real repository operations a database could do atomically.

What Chapter 12 already taught you. If you typed the ride-sharing chapter, most of the dispatch here will feel familiar. You get the same moves: a matching strategy over nearby candidates, an offer that expires, re-offering to the next candidate, an injected clock to drive the timeout, observers for trip events, and a policy object for cancellation money. The differences are worth saying out loud in an interview. Ride-sharing has two parties; food delivery has three, and the restaurant's accept is what starts dispatch, partway through the lifecycle. Ride-sharing charges the rider a cancellation fee; here the money flows the other way as a refund. A ride's price is distance times a surge multiplier; a food bill is a multi-step pipeline with coupons. And the ETA here is max(prep, ride to restaurant) plus the ride to the customer, because the partner travels while the kitchen cooks.

Type food_delivery/status.py:

"""The order lifecycle as data: statuses, actors, and the legal moves.

The transition table is the whole state machine. Order.advance() consults it
and nothing else, so adding a status is an edit to this table, not a hunt
through if-statements scattered across the service.
"""
from enum import Enum


class OrderStatus(Enum):
    PLACED = "placed"
    ACCEPTED = "accepted"
    PREPARING = "preparing"
    READY = "ready"
    PICKED_UP = "picked_up"
    DELIVERED = "delivered"
    REJECTED = "rejected"
    CANCELLED = "cancelled"


class Actor(Enum):
    """Who asked for a change. Refund rules depend on it."""
    CUSTOMER = "customer"
    RESTAURANT = "restaurant"
    PARTNER = "partner"
    PLATFORM = "platform"


TRANSITIONS: dict[OrderStatus, frozenset[OrderStatus]] = {
    OrderStatus.PLACED: frozenset({OrderStatus.ACCEPTED, OrderStatus.REJECTED,
                                   OrderStatus.CANCELLED}),
    OrderStatus.ACCEPTED: frozenset({OrderStatus.PREPARING, OrderStatus.CANCELLED}),
    OrderStatus.PREPARING: frozenset({OrderStatus.READY, OrderStatus.CANCELLED}),
    OrderStatus.READY: frozenset({OrderStatus.PICKED_UP, OrderStatus.CANCELLED}),
    OrderStatus.PICKED_UP: frozenset({OrderStatus.DELIVERED}),
    OrderStatus.DELIVERED: frozenset(),
    OrderStatus.REJECTED: frozenset(),
    OrderStatus.CANCELLED: frozenset(),
}

TERMINAL: frozenset[OrderStatus] = frozenset(
    status for status, targets in TRANSITIONS.items() if not targets)


def can_transition(current: OrderStatus, target: OrderStatus) -> bool:
    return target in TRANSITIONS[current]

What to notice. TERMINAL is derived from the table, not typed by hand. If you add a status with no exits, it becomes terminal automatically. Note also that READY → CANCELLED is legal in the table. Whether a customer may do it is a refund-policy question, answered later. The table says what can ever happen; the policy says who may make it happen.

Type food_delivery/config.py:

"""Configuration: every number the food-delivery domain depends on.

Money is Decimal, never float: a bill is added, discounted, taxed and
refunded, and float dust would show up on the customer's receipt.
Each group validates itself, so a bad deployment fails at startup.
"""
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from datetime import timedelta
from decimal import Decimal

from .status import OrderStatus


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class PricingConfig:
    tax_rate: Decimal = Decimal("0.05")            # charged on food after discounts
    base_delivery_fee: Decimal = Decimal("25.00")
    per_km_fee: Decimal = Decimal("8.00")

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if not Decimal(0) <= self.tax_rate < Decimal(1):
            raise ValueError("tax_rate must be in [0, 1)")
        if self.base_delivery_fee < 0 or self.per_km_fee < 0:
            raise ValueError("delivery fees must not be negative")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class DispatchConfig:
    offer_timeout: timedelta = timedelta(seconds=30)
    max_offer_attempts: int = 3
    max_pickup_radius_km: float = 5.0

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if self.offer_timeout <= timedelta(0):
            raise ValueError("offer_timeout must be positive")
        if self.max_offer_attempts < 1:
            raise ValueError("max_offer_attempts must be at least 1")
        if self.max_pickup_radius_km <= 0:
            raise ValueError("max_pickup_radius_km must be positive")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class EtaConfig:
    rider_speed_kmph: float = 20.0
    buffer_minutes: int = 5

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if self.rider_speed_kmph <= 0:
            raise ValueError("rider_speed_kmph must be positive")
        if self.buffer_minutes < 0:
            raise ValueError("buffer_minutes must not be negative")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class RefundConfig:
    """Fraction of the bill a customer gets back when *they* cancel.

    A status missing from the map means the customer can no longer cancel.
    Restaurant rejections and platform cancellations always refund in full;
    that rule lives in the refund policy, not here.
    """
    customer_refund_fraction: dict[OrderStatus, Decimal] = field(
        default_factory=lambda: {
            OrderStatus.PLACED: Decimal(1),
            OrderStatus.ACCEPTED: Decimal(1),
            OrderStatus.PREPARING: Decimal("0.5"),
        })

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        for status, fraction in self.customer_refund_fraction.items():
            if not Decimal(0) <= fraction <= Decimal(1):
                raise ValueError(f"refund fraction for {status.name} must be in [0, 1]")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class FoodDeliveryConfig:
    pricing: PricingConfig = field(default_factory=PricingConfig)
    dispatch: DispatchConfig = field(default_factory=DispatchConfig)
    eta: EtaConfig = field(default_factory=EtaConfig)
    refunds: RefundConfig = field(default_factory=RefundConfig)
    max_quantity_per_line: int = 20

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if self.max_quantity_per_line < 1:
            raise ValueError("max_quantity_per_line must be at least 1")

What to notice. Money is Decimal built from strings or ints, never from floats. Decimal(0.05) would carry the float's binary error into every tax line. The refund map also doubles as the "may the customer cancel?" rule: a status missing from the map means no. One structure, two answers, and they can't drift apart.

Step 3 — The clock

Goal: type the injectable clock. Every datetime in this chapter is timezone-aware, in UTC.

Type food_delivery/clock.py:

"""Injectable clock. Opening hours, offer timeouts and ETAs all read time,
so domain code asks a Clock and tests freeze it with FixedClock.
Every datetime is timezone-aware, in UTC."""
import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from datetime import UTC, datetime, timedelta


class Clock(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def now(self) -> datetime:
        ...


class SystemClock(Clock):
    def now(self) -> datetime:
        return datetime.now(UTC)


class FixedClock(Clock):
    """A clock that moves only when told to. The lock guards _now, because
    the concurrency tests read it from several threads at once."""

    def __init__(self, now: datetime):
        self._lock = threading.Lock()
        self._now = now

    def now(self) -> datetime:
        with self._lock:
            return self._now

    def advance(self, **delta: float) -> None:
        """advance(seconds=31), advance(minutes=5): any timedelta keyword."""
        with self._lock:
            self._now += timedelta(**delta)

What to notice. advance(**delta) forwards any timedelta keyword, so tests say advance(seconds=31) or advance(minutes=18) and read naturally. Unlike the car-rental clock, this one has a lock. The concurrency tests read it from several threads, and a test-only class still shouldn't race.

Type food_delivery/models.py:

"""Domain models: places, menus, partners, bills and orders.

Each model validates its own fields, so no half-built object ever exists.
Order.advance() is the only way an order's status changes, and it obeys the
transition table in status.py.
"""
import math
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from datetime import UTC, datetime, time, tzinfo
from decimal import ROUND_HALF_UP, Decimal

from .errors import InvalidTransitionError, MenuItemNotFoundError
from .status import Actor, OrderStatus, can_transition

CENT = Decimal("0.01")


def to_money(value: Decimal | int | str) -> Decimal:
    """Round to cents, half-up, the way a receipt does."""
    return Decimal(value).quantize(CENT, rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP)


def _require_text(value: str, name: str) -> None:
    if not value or not value.strip():
        raise ValueError(f"{name} must not be blank")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Location:
    """A point on a flat city grid, in kilometres. Good enough for an
    interview; a real system would call a routing service."""
    x_km: float
    y_km: float

    def distance_to(self, other: "Location") -> float:
        return math.hypot(self.x_km - other.x_km, self.y_km - other.y_km)


@dataclass
class MenuItem:
    item_id: str
    name: str
    price: Decimal
    available: bool = True

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        _require_text(self.item_id, "item_id")
        _require_text(self.name, "name")
        if self.price <= 0:
            raise ValueError(f"price of {self.item_id} must be positive")
        self.price = to_money(self.price)


@dataclass
class Restaurant:
    restaurant_id: str
    name: str
    location: Location
    opens_at: time
    closes_at: time
    prep_minutes: int
    menu: dict[str, MenuItem] = field(default_factory=dict)
    paused: bool = False
    tz: tzinfo = UTC                    # opening hours are local to the restaurant

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        _require_text(self.restaurant_id, "restaurant_id")
        _require_text(self.name, "name")
        if self.closes_at <= self.opens_at:
            raise ValueError("closes_at must be after opens_at")
        if self.prep_minutes < 0:
            raise ValueError("prep_minutes must not be negative")

    def is_open(self, at: datetime) -> bool:
        """Half-open hours, [opens_at, closes_at): at 23:00 sharp it is shut."""
        local = at.astimezone(self.tz).time()
        return not self.paused and self.opens_at <= local < self.closes_at

    def item(self, item_id: str) -> MenuItem:
        try:
            return self.menu[item_id]
        except KeyError:
            raise MenuItemNotFoundError(
                f"{self.restaurant_id} has no item {item_id}") from None


@dataclass
class DeliveryPartner:
    partner_id: str
    name: str
    location: Location
    available: bool = True

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        _require_text(self.partner_id, "partner_id")
        _require_text(self.name, "name")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class OrderLine:
    """A priced snapshot of one cart entry, frozen at checkout."""
    item_id: str
    name: str
    unit_price: Decimal
    quantity: int

    @property
    def line_total(self) -> Decimal:
        return to_money(self.unit_price * self.quantity)


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Bill:
    item_total: Decimal
    discount: Decimal
    delivery_fee: Decimal
    tax: Decimal
    total: Decimal
    notes: tuple[str, ...] = ()

class StatusChange:
    status: OrderStatus
    at: datetime
    actor: Actor


@dataclass
class Order:
    order_id: str
    customer_id: str
    restaurant_id: str
    lines: tuple[OrderLine, ...]
    bill: Bill
    drop: Location
    coupon_code: str | None
    idempotency_key: str
    placed_at: datetime
    eta: datetime
    status: OrderStatus = OrderStatus.PLACED
    partner_id: str | None = None
    refund: Decimal = Decimal("0.00")
    history: list[StatusChange] = field(default_factory=list)

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if not self.history:
            self.history.append(
                StatusChange(OrderStatus.PLACED, self.placed_at, Actor.CUSTOMER))

    def advance(self, target: OrderStatus, at: datetime, actor: Actor) -> None:
        if not can_transition(self.status, target):
            raise InvalidTransitionError(
                f"order {self.order_id}: {self.status.name} -> {target.name} "
                f"is not allowed")
        self.status = target
        self.history.append(StatusChange(target, at, actor))

At 23:00 sharp the restaurant is shut, with no special case. is_open also converts to the restaurant's own timezone first, because "opens at 10" means 10 local time, not 10 UTC. And OrderLine is frozen: it's a snapshot of the menu at checkout, so a later price change can't rewrite an order that's already been paid for.

Step 6 — The cart and the single-restaurant rule

Goal: type the Cart. The rule lives here, in add(), and nowhere else.

Type food_delivery/cart.py:

"""The cart: what a customer wants, from exactly one restaurant.

The cart stores item ids and quantities only. Prices are read from the menu
at checkout, so a price change between "add" and "pay" can never leave a
stale number on the bill.

Not thread-safe on its own: the service owns every cart and touches them
only while holding its lock.
"""
from .errors import InvalidQuantityError, MixedRestaurantCartError


class Cart:
    def __init__(self, customer_id: str, max_quantity_per_line: int):
        self.customer_id = customer_id
        self._max_quantity = max_quantity_per_line
        self._restaurant_id: str | None = None
        self._quantities: dict[str, int] = {}

    @property
    def restaurant_id(self) -> str | None:
        return self._restaurant_id

    @property
    def is_empty(self) -> bool:
        return not self._quantities

    def items(self) -> dict[str, int]:
        return dict(self._quantities)

    def add(self, restaurant_id: str, item_id: str, quantity: int,
            *, replace: bool = False) -> None:
        if quantity < 1:
            raise InvalidQuantityError(f"quantity must be at least 1, got {quantity}")
        if self._restaurant_id not in (None, restaurant_id):
            if not replace:
                raise MixedRestaurantCartError(
                    f"cart holds items from {self._restaurant_id}; "
                    f"clear it or pass replace=True to order from {restaurant_id}")
            self.clear()
        new_quantity = self._quantities.get(item_id, 0) + quantity
        if new_quantity > self._max_quantity:
            raise InvalidQuantityError(
                f"at most {self._max_quantity} of one item per order")
        self._restaurant_id = restaurant_id
        self._quantities[item_id] = new_quantity

    def remove(self, item_id: str, quantity: int | None = None) -> None:
        """Remove some (or, with quantity=None, all) of one item."""
        current = self._quantities.get(item_id, 0)
        if current == 0:
            return
        if quantity is None or quantity >= current:
            del self._quantities[item_id]
        elif quantity < 1:
            raise InvalidQuantityError(f"quantity must be at least 1, got {quantity}")
        else:
            self._quantities[item_id] = current - quantity
        if not self._quantities:
            self._restaurant_id = None

    def clear(self) -> None:
        self._quantities.clear()
        self._restaurant_id = None

What to notice. Removing the last item resets restaurant_id to None. Without that line, a customer who empties their cart would still be locked to the old restaurant. The cart has no lock of its own. It is never shared: the service owns all carts and touches them only while holding its lock. The docstring says so, which is the "documented" half of the thread-safety rule.

"""Pricing as a pipeline of steps, with discounts as a chain of rules.

    item total -> delivery fee -> discount chain -> tax -> Bill

Each step reads and writes one PricingContext. The order of steps is the
business rule ("tax is charged after discounts"), and it is visible in one
list instead of buried in one long function.

Discounts are a Chain of Responsibility. Every rule sees the context and
decides for itself whether it applies. A coupon rule acts only when the
code is its own, and only if no other coupon got there first.
"""
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from collections.abc import Sequence
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from decimal import Decimal
from itertools import pairwise

from .config import PricingConfig
from .errors import CouponNotApplicableError, InvalidCouponError
from .models import Bill, OrderLine, to_money

ZERO = Decimal("0.00")


@dataclass
class PricingContext:
    lines: tuple[OrderLine, ...]
    distance_km: float
    coupon_code: str | None
    item_total: Decimal = ZERO
    delivery_fee: Decimal = ZERO
    discount: Decimal = ZERO
    tax: Decimal = ZERO
    coupon_applied: bool = False
    notes: list[str] = field(default_factory=list)

    @property
    def food_after_discount(self) -> Decimal:
        return self.item_total - self.discount


# -- discount rules: the chain ----------------------------------------

class DiscountRule(ABC):
    """One link. handle() applies this rule, then passes the context on."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._next: DiscountRule | None = None

    def then(self, rule: "DiscountRule") -> "DiscountRule":
        """Link rule after this one; returns rule, so calls chain left to right."""
        self._next = rule
        return rule

    def handle(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        self.apply(ctx)
        if self._next is not None:
            self._next.handle(ctx)

    @abstractmethod
    def apply(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        ...


class CouponRule(DiscountRule):
    """Template for coupon codes: match, check minimum spend, then discount."""

    def __init__(self, code: str, min_item_total: Decimal = ZERO):
        super().__init__()
        self.code = code.strip().upper()
        self.min_item_total = to_money(min_item_total)

    def apply(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        if ctx.coupon_applied or ctx.coupon_code != self.code:
            return
        if ctx.item_total < self.min_item_total:
            raise CouponNotApplicableError(
                f"{self.code} needs a food total of at least {self.min_item_total}")
        amount = min(self.discount_for(ctx), ctx.food_after_discount)
        ctx.discount += amount
        ctx.coupon_applied = True
        ctx.notes.append(f"{self.code}: -{amount}")

    @abstractmethod
    def discount_for(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> Decimal:
        ...


class PercentOffCoupon(CouponRule):
    def __init__(self, code: str, percent: Decimal, cap: Decimal,
                 min_item_total: Decimal = ZERO):
        super().__init__(code, min_item_total)
        if not Decimal(0) < percent <= Decimal(100):
            raise ValueError("percent must be in (0, 100]")
        self.percent = percent
        self.cap = to_money(cap)

    def discount_for(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> Decimal:
        return min(to_money(ctx.item_total * self.percent / 100), self.cap)


class FlatOffCoupon(CouponRule):
    def __init__(self, code: str, amount: Decimal, min_item_total: Decimal = ZERO):
        super().__init__(code, min_item_total)
        if amount <= 0:
            raise ValueError("amount must be positive")
        self.amount = to_money(amount)

    def discount_for(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> Decimal:
        return self.amount


class FreeDeliveryAbove(DiscountRule):
    """Automatic, no code needed. Put it after the coupons in the chain:
    the threshold is checked against food *after* the coupon."""

    def __init__(self, threshold: Decimal):
        super().__init__()
        self.threshold = to_money(threshold)

    def apply(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        if ctx.delivery_fee > 0 and ctx.food_after_discount >= self.threshold:
            ctx.notes.append(f"free delivery over {self.threshold}: -{ctx.delivery_fee}")
            ctx.delivery_fee = ZERO


def chain(*rules: DiscountRule) -> DiscountRule | None:
    """chain(a, b, c) links a -> b -> c and returns a (or None if empty)."""
    for current, following in pairwise(rules):
        current.then(following)
    return rules[0] if rules else None


# -- pipeline steps ----------------------------------------------------

class PricingStep(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def apply(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        ...


class ItemTotalStep(PricingStep):
    def apply(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        ctx.item_total = to_money(sum((line.line_total for line in ctx.lines), ZERO))


class DeliveryFeeStep(PricingStep):
    def __init__(self, config: PricingConfig):
        self._config = config

    def apply(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        distance = Decimal(str(round(ctx.distance_km, 2)))
        ctx.delivery_fee = to_money(
            self._config.base_delivery_fee + self._config.per_km_fee * distance)


class DiscountStep(PricingStep):
    def __init__(self, rules: DiscountRule | None):
        self._rules = rules

    def apply(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        if self._rules is not None:
            self._rules.handle(ctx)
        if ctx.coupon_code is not None and not ctx.coupon_applied:
            raise InvalidCouponError(f"unknown coupon {ctx.coupon_code}")


class TaxStep(PricingStep):
    def __init__(self, config: PricingConfig):
        self._config = config

    def apply(self, ctx: PricingContext) -> None:
        ctx.tax = to_money(ctx.food_after_discount * self._config.tax_rate)


class PricingPipeline:
    def __init__(self, steps: Sequence[PricingStep]):
        if not steps:
            raise ValueError("a pricing pipeline needs at least one step")
        self._steps = tuple(steps)

    @classmethod
    def standard(cls, config: PricingConfig,
                 discounts: DiscountRule | None = None) -> "PricingPipeline":
        return cls([ItemTotalStep(), DeliveryFeeStep(config),
                    DiscountStep(discounts), TaxStep(config)])

    def price(self, lines: tuple[OrderLine, ...], distance_km: float,
              coupon_code: str | None = None) -> Bill:
        code = coupon_code.strip().upper() if coupon_code else None
        ctx = PricingContext(lines=lines, distance_km=distance_km, coupon_code=code)
        for step in self._steps:
            step.apply(ctx)
        total = ctx.food_after_discount + ctx.delivery_fee + ctx.tax
        return Bill(item_total=ctx.item_total, discount=to_money(ctx.discount),
                    delivery_fee=ctx.delivery_fee, tax=ctx.tax,
                    total=to_money(total), notes=tuple(ctx.notes))

    def add(self, partner: DeliveryPartner) -> None: ...
    @abstractmethod
    def get(self, partner_id: str) -> DeliveryPartner | None: ...
    @abstractmethod
    def available(self) -> list[DeliveryPartner]: ...
    @abstractmethod

        """Mark the partner busy if they are free. True if this call won."""
    @abstractmethod
    def release(self, partner_id: str) -> None: ...


class InMemoryPartners(PartnerRepository):
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()
        self._partners: dict[str, DeliveryPartner] = {}

    def add(self, partner: DeliveryPartner) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._partners[partner.partner_id] = partner

    def get(self, partner_id: str) -> DeliveryPartner | None:
        with self._lock:
            return self._partners.get(partner_id)

    def available(self) -> list[DeliveryPartner]:
        with self._lock:
            return [p for p in self._partners.values() if p.available]

    def reserve(self, partner_id: str) -> bool:
        with self._lock:
            partner = self._partners.get(partner_id)
            if partner is None or not partner.available:
                return False
            partner.available = False
            return True

    def release(self, partner_id: str) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            partner = self._partners.get(partner_id)
            if partner is not None:
                partner.available = True


class OrderRepository(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def next_id(self) -> str: ...
    @abstractmethod
    def add(self, order: Order) -> None: ...
    @abstractmethod
    def get(self, order_id: str) -> Order | None: ...
    @abstractmethod
    def save(self, order: Order) -> None: ...
    @abstractmethod
    def find_by_key(self, customer_id: str, idempotency_key: str) -> Order | None: ...
    @abstractmethod
    def all(self) -> list[Order]: ...


class InMemoryOrders(OrderRepository):
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()
        self._ids = itertools.count(1)
        self._orders: dict[str, Order] = {}
        self._by_key: dict[tuple[str, str], str] = {}

    def next_id(self) -> str:
        with self._lock:
            return f"ORD-{next(self._ids):04d}"

    def add(self, order: Order) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._orders[order.order_id] = order
            self._by_key[(order.customer_id, order.idempotency_key)] = order.order_id

    def get(self, order_id: str) -> Order | None:
        with self._lock:
            return self._orders.get(order_id)

    def save(self, order: Order) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._orders[order.order_id] = order

    def find_by_key(self, customer_id: str, idempotency_key: str) -> Order | None:
        with self._lock:
            order_id = self._by_key.get((customer_id, idempotency_key))
            return self._orders.get(order_id) if order_id else None

    def all(self) -> list[Order]:
        with self._lock:
            return list(self._orders.values())

Step 9 — Dispatch: strategy, offers, timeouts

Goal: type the assignment strategy and the Dispatcher that runs the offer loop.

Type food_delivery/dispatch.py:

"""Dispatch: offer an order to a delivery partner, and re-offer on timeout.

The strategy answers "which partner?". The dispatcher owns the offer's life:
reserve a partner, wait for an answer, and on decline or timeout release
them and try the next one. After max_offer_attempts it gives up and
reports FAILED; the service decides what failure means for the order.

Lock order: the service lock, then this lock, then the partner repo's lock.
The dispatcher never calls back into the service, so it cannot deadlock.
"""
import logging
import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from collections.abc import Sequence
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from datetime import datetime
from enum import Enum

from .clock import Clock
from .config import DispatchConfig
from .errors import NoActiveOfferError, PartnerMismatchError
from .models import DeliveryPartner, Location
from .repositories import PartnerRepository

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)


class AssignmentStrategy(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def choose(self, pickup: Location,
               candidates: Sequence[DeliveryPartner]) -> DeliveryPartner | None:
        ...


class NearestPartnerStrategy(AssignmentStrategy):
    """Closest free partner within the radius. Ties go to the lower id, so
    the choice is deterministic and the tests can pin it."""

    def __init__(self, max_radius_km: float):
        self._max_radius_km = max_radius_km

    def choose(self, pickup: Location,
               candidates: Sequence[DeliveryPartner]) -> DeliveryPartner | None:
        in_range = [p for p in candidates
                    if p.location.distance_to(pickup) <= self._max_radius_km]
        if not in_range:
            return None
        return min(in_range,
                   key=lambda p: (p.location.distance_to(pickup), p.partner_id))


class DispatchResult(Enum):
    OFFERED = "offered"      # a partner holds the offer and must answer
    WAITING = "waiting"      # nobody free in range; retry after the timeout
    FAILED = "failed"        # attempts exhausted; the order needs a decision


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class DispatchOutcome:
    order_id: str
    result: DispatchResult
    partner_id: str | None
    attempt: int


@dataclass
class _Offer:
    order_id: str
    pickup: Location
    partner_id: str | None = None
    offered_at: datetime | None = None
    attempts: int = 0
    accepted: bool = False
    tried: set[str] = field(default_factory=set)


class Dispatcher:
    def __init__(self, partners: PartnerRepository, strategy: AssignmentStrategy,
                 clock: Clock, config: DispatchConfig):
        self._partners = partners
        self._strategy = strategy
        self._clock = clock
        self._config = config
        self._lock = threading.Lock()          # guards _offers
        self._offers: dict[str, _Offer] = {}

    def start(self, order_id: str, pickup: Location) -> DispatchOutcome:
        with self._lock:
            offer = _Offer(order_id=order_id, pickup=pickup)
            self._offers[order_id] = offer
            return self._next_offer(offer)

    def accept(self, order_id: str, partner_id: str) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            offer = self._pending_offer_for(order_id, partner_id)
            offer.accepted = True
            log.info("partner %s accepted %s", partner_id, order_id)

    def decline(self, order_id: str, partner_id: str) -> DispatchOutcome:
        with self._lock:
            offer = self._pending_offer_for(order_id, partner_id)
            log.info("partner %s declined %s", partner_id, order_id)
            return self._next_offer(offer)

    def expire_stale(self) -> list[DispatchOutcome]:
        """Re-offer every unanswered offer older than the timeout."""
        now = self._clock.now()
        outcomes = []
        with self._lock:
            for offer in list(self._offers.values()):
                if offer.accepted or offer.offered_at is None:
                    continue
                if now - offer.offered_at >= self._config.offer_timeout:
                    log.info("offer for %s timed out (partner %s)",
                             offer.order_id, offer.partner_id)
                    outcomes.append(self._next_offer(offer))
        return outcomes

    def finish(self, order_id: str) -> None:
        """Delivered or cancelled: free the partner and forget the offer."""
        with self._lock:
            offer = self._offers.pop(order_id, None)
            if offer is not None and offer.partner_id is not None:
                self._partners.release(offer.partner_id)

    def assigned_partner(self, order_id: str) -> str | None:
        with self._lock:
            offer = self._offers.get(order_id)
            return offer.partner_id if offer and offer.accepted else None

    # -- helpers (caller holds self._lock) ------------------------------

    def _pending_offer_for(self, order_id: str, partner_id: str) -> _Offer:
        offer = self._offers.get(order_id)
        if offer is None or offer.accepted:
            raise NoActiveOfferError(f"no open offer for {order_id}")
        if offer.partner_id != partner_id:
            raise PartnerMismatchError(
                f"{order_id} is offered to {offer.partner_id}, not {partner_id}")
        return offer

    def _next_offer(self, offer: _Offer) -> DispatchOutcome:
        if offer.partner_id is not None:
            self._partners.release(offer.partner_id)
            offer.tried.add(offer.partner_id)
            offer.partner_id = None
        offer.attempts += 1
        if offer.attempts > self._config.max_offer_attempts:
            del self._offers[offer.order_id]
            log.warning("dispatch failed for %s after %d attempts",
                        offer.order_id, offer.attempts - 1)
            return DispatchOutcome(offer.order_id, DispatchResult.FAILED,
                                   None, offer.attempts - 1)
        offer.partner_id = self._claim_partner(offer)
        offer.offered_at = self._clock.now()
        result = (DispatchResult.OFFERED if offer.partner_id
                  else DispatchResult.WAITING)
        log.info("dispatch %s attempt %d: %s %s", offer.order_id,
                 offer.attempts, result.value, offer.partner_id or "")
        return DispatchOutcome(offer.order_id, result, offer.partner_id,
                               offer.attempts)

    def _claim_partner(self, offer: _Offer) -> str | None:
        candidates = [p for p in self._partners.available()
                      if p.partner_id not in offer.tried]
        while candidates:
            choice = self._strategy.choose(offer.pickup, candidates)
            if choice is None:
                return None
            if self._partners.reserve(choice.partner_id):
                return choice.partner_id
            candidates = [p for p in candidates if p.partner_id != choice.partner_id]
        return None

What to notice. _next_offer is the whole loop in one method. It releases the current partner, remembers them in tried, bumps the attempt count, and either gives up or claims someone new. Decline and timeout both call it, so they behave the same by construction. _claim_partner handles the race where the strategy picks someone who was grabbed a microsecond earlier: reserve fails, that partner is dropped from the candidates, and the strategy picks again.

An offer with nobody in range is WAITING, not failed. It still gets an offered_at and still counts as an attempt, so the next tick() after the timeout retries it. Riders come online all the time.

Step 10 — The ETA seam

Goal: type the EtaEstimator interface and a distance-based implementation.

Type food_delivery/eta.py:

"""ETA as a seam. The service asks "how long?"; the estimator decides.

Today it is distance over speed. Tomorrow it is a model trained on traffic
and kitchen load. Either way the service does not change.
"""
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from datetime import timedelta

from .config import EtaConfig
from .models import Location, Restaurant


class EtaEstimator(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def estimate(self, restaurant: Restaurant, drop: Location,
                 partner_at: Location | None) -> timedelta:
        ...


class DistanceEtaEstimator(EtaEstimator):
    """The partner rides to the restaurant while the kitchen cooks, so the
    wait at the restaurant is the slower of the two, then the ride to drop."""

    def __init__(self, config: EtaConfig):
        self._config = config

    def _ride_minutes(self, a: Location, b: Location) -> float:
        return a.distance_to(b) / self._config.rider_speed_kmph * 60

    def estimate(self, restaurant: Restaurant, drop: Location,
                 partner_at: Location | None) -> timedelta:
        to_restaurant = (self._ride_minutes(partner_at, restaurant.location)
                         if partner_at is not None else 0.0)
        wait = max(float(restaurant.prep_minutes), to_restaurant)
        ride = self._ride_minutes(restaurant.location, drop)
        return timedelta(minutes=round(wait + ride + self._config.buffer_minutes))

"""Refund rules: who cancelled, and how far the food got.

A customer who cancels before cooking starts gets everything back. Once the
kitchen is cooking they get half. After the food is ready they cannot
cancel at all. A restaurant rejection or a platform failure is never the
customer's fault, so those always refund in full.
"""
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from decimal import Decimal

from .config import RefundConfig
from .models import Order, to_money
from .status import Actor, OrderStatus


class RefundPolicy(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def customer_may_cancel(self, status: OrderStatus) -> bool:
        ...

    @abstractmethod
    def refund_for(self, order: Order, actor: Actor) -> Decimal:
        """Amount to return when actor ends the order in its current status."""


class StandardRefundPolicy(RefundPolicy):
    def __init__(self, config: RefundConfig):
        self._fractions = config.customer_refund_fraction

    def customer_may_cancel(self, status: OrderStatus) -> bool:
        return status in self._fractions

    def refund_for(self, order: Order, actor: Actor) -> Decimal:
        if actor is Actor.CUSTOMER:
            fraction = self._fractions.get(order.status, Decimal(0))
        else:
            fraction = Decimal(1)
        return to_money(order.bill.total * fraction)

What to notice. The policy never changes the order. It only answers two questions: may the customer cancel now, and how much goes back. The service does the transition. That split means a "cancellation fee after 2 minutes" rule is a new policy class, and the state machine never hears about it.

"""Notifications through observers.

The service publishes OrderEvents; observers (push, SMS, the restaurant
tablet, analytics) subscribe. The service never knows who is listening.
A failing observer is logged and skipped: a broken SMS gateway must not
roll back a delivered order.

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)


class EventKind(Enum):
    STATUS_CHANGED = "status_changed"
    PARTNER_OFFERED = "partner_offered"
    PARTNER_ASSIGNED = "partner_assigned"
    DISPATCH_WAITING = "dispatch_waiting"
    DISPATCH_FAILED = "dispatch_failed"


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class OrderEvent:
    kind: EventKind
    order_id: str
    customer_id: str
    status: OrderStatus
    at: datetime
    detail: str = ""


class OrderObserver(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def on_event(self, event: OrderEvent) -> None:
        ...


class LoggingObserver(OrderObserver):
    def on_event(self, event: OrderEvent) -> None:
        log.info("%s %s %s %s", event.order_id, event.kind.value,
                 event.status.name, event.detail)


class RecordingObserver(OrderObserver):
    """Keeps every event. Handy in tests and demos."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()
        self._events: list[OrderEvent] = []

    def on_event(self, event: OrderEvent) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._events.append(event)

    @property
    def events(self) -> list[OrderEvent]:
        with self._lock:
            return list(self._events)


class Notifier:
    def __init__(self, observers: Iterable[OrderObserver] = ()):
        self._lock = threading.Lock()          # guards _observers
        self._observers: list[OrderObserver] = list(observers)

    def subscribe(self, observer: OrderObserver) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._observers.append(observer)

    def publish(self, events: Iterable[OrderEvent]) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            observers = list(self._observers)
        for event in events:
            for observer in observers:
                try:
                    observer.on_event(event)
                except Exception:
                    # Isolation boundary: one bad observer must not stop the
                    # others or the order flow. Logged with the traceback.
                    log.exception("observer %r failed on %s",
                                  observer, event.kind.value)

What to notice. except Exception in publish is the one broad catch in the package, and it's deliberate. It is not a bare except:. It doesn't swallow KeyboardInterrupt, and it logs the full traceback with log.exception. This is an isolation boundary: code you don't own (an SMS client, an analytics SDK) must not be able to un-deliver an order. publish also copies the observer list under the lock and calls observers outside it, so an observer that subscribes another observer can't deadlock.

def _require_text(value: str, name: str) -> str:
    if not value or not value.strip():
        raise ValueError(f"{name} must not be blank")
    return value.strip()


class FoodDeliveryService:
    def __init__(
        self,
        *,
        clock: Clock | None = None,
        config: FoodDeliveryConfig | None = None,
        restaurants: RestaurantRepository | None = None,
        partners: PartnerRepository | None = None,
        orders: OrderRepository | None = None,
        discounts: DiscountRule | None = None,
        pricing: PricingPipeline | None = None,
        dispatcher: Dispatcher | None = None,
        eta: EtaEstimator | None = None,
        refunds: RefundPolicy | None = None,
        notifier: Notifier | None = None,
    ):
        self._clock = clock or SystemClock()
        self._config = config or FoodDeliveryConfig()
        self._restaurants = restaurants or InMemoryRestaurants()
        self._partners = partners or InMemoryPartners()
        self._orders = orders or InMemoryOrders()
        self._pricing = pricing or PricingPipeline.standard(
            self._config.pricing, discounts)
        self._dispatcher = dispatcher or Dispatcher(
            self._partners,
            NearestPartnerStrategy(self._config.dispatch.max_pickup_radius_km),
            self._clock, self._config.dispatch)
        self._eta = eta or DistanceEtaEstimator(self._config.eta)
        self._refunds = refunds or StandardRefundPolicy(self._config.refunds)
        self._notifier = notifier or Notifier()
        self._lock = threading.RLock()          # guards _carts and every order change
        self._carts: dict[str, Cart] = {}

    # -- catalogue ------------------------------------------------------

    def set_item_available(self, restaurant_id: str, item_id: str,
                           available: bool) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._restaurant(restaurant_id).item(item_id).available = available

    def set_paused(self, restaurant_id: str, paused: bool) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._restaurant(restaurant_id).paused = paused

    # -- cart -----------------------------------------------------------

    def add_to_cart(self, customer_id: str, restaurant_id: str, item_id: str,
                    quantity: int = 1, *, replace: bool = False) -> dict[str, int]:
        customer_id = _require_text(customer_id, "customer_id")
        with self._lock:
            item = self._restaurant(restaurant_id).item(item_id)
            if not item.available:
                raise ItemUnavailableError(f"{item.name} is not available right now")
            cart = self._carts.setdefault(
                customer_id, Cart(customer_id, self._config.max_quantity_per_line))
            cart.add(restaurant_id, item_id, quantity, replace=replace)
            return cart.items()

    def remove_from_cart(self, customer_id: str, item_id: str,
                         quantity: int | None = None) -> dict[str, int]:
        with self._lock:
            cart = self._carts.get(customer_id)
            if cart is None:
                return {}
            cart.remove(item_id, quantity)
            return cart.items()

    def cart(self, customer_id: str) -> dict[str, int]:
        with self._lock:
            cart = self._carts.get(customer_id)
            return cart.items() if cart else {}

    # -- checkout -------------------------------------------------------

    def quote(self, customer_id: str, drop: Location,
              coupon_code: str | None = None) -> Bill:
        with self._lock:
            restaurant, lines = self._checkout_lines(customer_id)
        return self._pricing.price(
            lines, restaurant.location.distance_to(drop), coupon_code)

    def place_order(self, customer_id: str, drop: Location, idempotency_key: str,
                    coupon_code: str | None = None) -> Order:
        """Safe to retry: the same key returns the same order, charged once."""
        customer_id = _require_text(customer_id, "customer_id")
        key = _require_text(idempotency_key, "idempotency_key")
        code = coupon_code.strip().upper() if coupon_code else None
        with self._lock:
            existing = self._orders.find_by_key(customer_id, key)
            if existing is not None:
                if (existing.drop, existing.coupon_code) != (drop, code):
                    raise IdempotencyKeyReusedError(
                        f"key {key} was used for a different request")
                log.info("replay of key %s -> %s", key, existing.order_id)
                return existing
            restaurant, lines = self._checkout_lines(customer_id)
            bill = self._pricing.price(
                lines, restaurant.location.distance_to(drop), code)
            now = self._clock.now()
            order = Order(
                order_id=self._orders.next_id(), customer_id=customer_id,
                restaurant_id=restaurant.restaurant_id, lines=lines, bill=bill,
                drop=drop, coupon_code=code, idempotency_key=key, placed_at=now,
                eta=now + self._eta.estimate(restaurant, drop, None))
            self._orders.add(order)
            self._carts[customer_id].clear()
            log.info("placed %s for %s, total %s", order.order_id,
                     customer_id, bill.total)
            events = [self._event(order, EventKind.STATUS_CHANGED)]
        self._notifier.publish(events)
        return order

    # -- restaurant side ------------------------------------------------

    def accept(self, order_id: str) -> Order:
        """The restaurant takes the order; dispatch starts right away, so a
        partner rides over while the kitchen cooks."""
        with self._lock:
            order = self._order(order_id)
            events = [self._move(order, OrderStatus.ACCEPTED, Actor.RESTAURANT)]
            pickup = self._restaurant(order.restaurant_id).location
            outcome = self._dispatcher.start(order.order_id, pickup)
            events += self._handle_dispatch(outcome)
        self._notifier.publish(events)
        return order

    def reject(self, order_id: str) -> Order:
        return self._end(order_id, OrderStatus.REJECTED, Actor.RESTAURANT)

    def start_preparing(self, order_id: str) -> Order:
        return self._step(order_id, OrderStatus.PREPARING, Actor.RESTAURANT)

    def mark_ready(self, order_id: str) -> Order:
        return self._step(order_id, OrderStatus.READY, Actor.RESTAURANT)

    # -- partner side ---------------------------------------------------

    def partner_accept(self, order_id: str, partner_id: str) -> Order:
        with self._lock:
            order = self._order(order_id)
            self._dispatcher.accept(order_id, partner_id)
            partner = self._partners.get(partner_id)
            if partner is None:
                raise PartnerNotFoundError(f"no partner {partner_id}")
            order.partner_id = partner_id
            restaurant = self._restaurant(order.restaurant_id)
            order.eta = self._clock.now() + self._eta.estimate(
                restaurant, order.drop, partner.location)
            self._orders.save(order)
            events = [self._event(order, EventKind.PARTNER_ASSIGNED,
                                  f"{partner.name}, eta {order.eta:%H:%M}")]
        self._notifier.publish(events)
        return order

    def partner_decline(self, order_id: str, partner_id: str) -> Order:
        with self._lock:
            order = self._order(order_id)
            events = self._handle_dispatch(
                self._dispatcher.decline(order_id, partner_id))
        self._notifier.publish(events)
        return order

    def pick_up(self, order_id: str, partner_id: str) -> Order:
        with self._lock:
            order = self._order(order_id)
            self._check_partner(order, partner_id)
            event = self._move(order, OrderStatus.PICKED_UP, Actor.PARTNER)
        self._notifier.publish([event])
        return order

    def deliver(self, order_id: str, partner_id: str) -> Order:
        with self._lock:
            order = self._order(order_id)
            self._check_partner(order, partner_id)
            event = self._move(order, OrderStatus.DELIVERED, Actor.PARTNER)
            self._dispatcher.finish(order_id)
        self._notifier.publish([event])
        return order

    # -- cancellation and housekeeping -----------------------------------

    def cancel(self, order_id: str, actor: Actor = Actor.CUSTOMER) -> Order:
        return self._end(order_id, OrderStatus.CANCELLED, actor)

    def tick(self) -> list[DispatchOutcome]:
        """Call on a schedule: re-offers timed-out offers, and cancels orders
        nobody would deliver."""
        with self._lock:
            outcomes = self._dispatcher.expire_stale()
            events = []
            for outcome in outcomes:
                events += self._handle_dispatch(outcome)
        self._notifier.publish(events)
        return outcomes

    def eta(self, order_id: str) -> datetime:
        with self._lock:
            return self._order(order_id).eta

    def get_order(self, order_id: str) -> Order:
        with self._lock:
            return self._order(order_id)

    # -- helpers --------------------------------------------------------

    def _restaurant(self, restaurant_id: str) -> Restaurant:
        restaurant = self._restaurants.get(restaurant_id)
        if restaurant is None:
            raise RestaurantNotFoundError(f"no restaurant {restaurant_id}")
        return restaurant

    def _order(self, order_id: str) -> Order:
        order = self._orders.get(order_id)
        if order is None:
            raise OrderNotFoundError(f"no order {order_id}")
        return order

    def _checkout_lines(self, customer_id: str) -> tuple[Restaurant, tuple[OrderLine, ...]]:
        """Re-validate the cart against the live menu. Caller holds the lock."""
        cart = self._carts.get(customer_id)
        if cart is None or cart.is_empty or cart.restaurant_id is None:
            raise EmptyCartError(f"cart for {customer_id} is empty")
        restaurant = self._restaurant(cart.restaurant_id)
        if not restaurant.is_open(self._clock.now()):
            raise RestaurantClosedError(f"{restaurant.name} is not taking orders")
        lines = []
        for item_id, quantity in cart.items().items():
            item = restaurant.item(item_id)
            if not item.available:
                raise ItemUnavailableError(f"{item.name} sold out; remove it to continue")
            lines.append(OrderLine(item.item_id, item.name, item.price, quantity))
        return restaurant, tuple(lines)

    def _check_partner(self, order: Order, partner_id: str) -> None:
        if order.partner_id is None or order.partner_id != partner_id:
            raise PartnerMismatchError(
                f"{order.order_id} is assigned to {order.partner_id}, not {partner_id}")

    def _event(self, order: Order, kind: EventKind, detail: str = "") -> OrderEvent:
        return OrderEvent(kind, order.order_id, order.customer_id, order.status,
                          self._clock.now(), detail)

    def _move(self, order: Order, target: OrderStatus, actor: Actor) -> OrderEvent:
        order.advance(target, self._clock.now(), actor)
        self._orders.save(order)
        log.info("%s -> %s by %s", order.order_id, target.name, actor.value)
        return self._event(order, EventKind.STATUS_CHANGED)

    def _step(self, order_id: str, target: OrderStatus, actor: Actor) -> Order:
        with self._lock:
            order = self._order(order_id)
            event = self._move(order, target, actor)
        self._notifier.publish([event])
        return order

    def _end(self, order_id: str, target: OrderStatus, actor: Actor) -> Order:
        with self._lock:
            order = self._order(order_id)
            events = self._end_locked(order, target, actor)
        self._notifier.publish(events)
        return order

    def _end_locked(self, order: Order, target: OrderStatus,
                    actor: Actor) -> list[OrderEvent]:
        if actor is Actor.CUSTOMER and not self._refunds.customer_may_cancel(order.status):
            raise CancellationNotAllowedError(
                f"{order.order_id} is {order.status.name}; too late to cancel")
        refund = self._refunds.refund_for(order, actor)
        event = self._move(order, target, actor)
        order.refund = refund
        self._dispatcher.finish(order.order_id)
        log.info("%s ended by %s, refund %s", order.order_id, actor.value, refund)
        return [event]

    def _handle_dispatch(self, outcome: DispatchOutcome) -> list[OrderEvent]:
        order = self._order(outcome.order_id)
        events = [self._event(order, _DISPATCH_EVENTS[outcome.result],
                              f"attempt {outcome.attempt} {outcome.partner_id or ''}".strip())]
        if outcome.result is DispatchResult.FAILED:
            events += self._end_locked(order, OrderStatus.CANCELLED, Actor.PLATFORM)
        return events

What to notice. Look at the shape every public method shares: take the lock, load, check, move, collect events, release, publish. place_order follows it with one twist. The idempotency lookup comes before _checkout_lines, because a retry finds an empty cart.

The lock order is documented and one-directional: service lock, then dispatcher lock, then repository locks. Nothing lower ever calls up, so no cycle exists.

tick() is where a dispatch failure turns into a cancellation. The dispatcher reports FAILED, and the service cancels as Actor.PLATFORM. The refund policy then pays back in full without any special case.

            target(i)

9. Common interview follow-ups

"Now let a customer order from two restaurants in one checkout." Don't relax the cart rule. Add a level above it. A Checkout (or "group order") holds one Cart per restaurant and splits into one Order per restaurant, linked by a checkout_id. Pricing runs per order, and a checkout-level step can spread one delivery fee or one coupon across them. Each order keeps its own state machine and its own partner, unless you also do batching (next question). The single-restaurant invariant survives, because it was always really "one order, one kitchen."

"Now batch two orders onto one rider." Today the dispatcher maps one order to one offer. Batching makes an offer hold a list of order ids. Add a BatchingStrategy that, before offering, looks for another READY-soon order from the same restaurant or one nearby, going in a similar direction. PartnerRepository.reserve doesn't change; a partner still gets claimed once. pick_up and deliver already take a partner id and check it per order, so they work unchanged. One edit: finish must release the partner only when the last order in the batch is delivered. The ETA estimator gets a route-aware implementation, since the second drop waits for the first.

"Now run three app servers behind a load balancer." Every in-memory lock stops being enough. Three changes: - Idempotency: a unique constraint on (customer_id, idempotency_key) in the orders table. Insert first; on a duplicate-key error, read and return the existing row. - Partner claim: reserve becomes the conditional UPDATE ... WHERE available = true from Step 8, checked by row count. - Order transitions: optimistic locking with a version column, so UPDATE orders SET status = ?, version = version + 1 WHERE id = ? AND version = ?.

tick() runs on one leader, or each server claims expired offers with SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED. Notifications go through an outbox table so an event is published only if its transaction committed. The domain classes don't change; the repositories do. That's the payoff of keeping those operations on the repository interface.

"Now add a rain surge on the delivery fee." A new PricingStep, SurgeDeliveryStep, placed right after DeliveryFeeStep in the list. It reads a SurgePolicy (borrow the one from Chapter 12) and multiplies the fee. The free-delivery rule still runs after it in the discount chain, so the business decides whether "free delivery" covers surge by where they put one line. No existing class is edited.

"Now support scheduled orders: lunch tomorrow at 1 pm." Add a SCHEDULED status before PLACED, with SCHEDULED → PLACED and SCHEDULED → CANCELLED in the table. place_order takes an optional deliver_at. Opening-hours validation checks deliver_at minus prep time instead of now. tick() gains one more job: promote scheduled orders whose send time has come. Refund rules gain a row for SCHEDULED (full refund). Every piece lands in an existing seam.

"Why is the timeout a tick() and not a threading.Timer per offer?" Timers hide threads inside the domain. Then every test becomes a sleep, and a crashed process silently loses its pending timers. A tick() called by a scheduler is testable with a frozen clock, and it moves to a cron job or a delayed-queue consumer without changing the dispatcher. It is the same reason Chapter 12 drives its offer expiry from the clock.

"Why Decimal here when Chapter 6 used float?" Car rental multiplied a rate by days and rounded once. A food bill adds, takes a percentage, caps, taxes, and later refunds half. Each step is a chance for float dust, and the refund must match the charge to the paisa. Decimal with explicit half-up rounding gives the exact receipt a finance team would audit.

"What if the restaurant never accepts?" Same shape as the partner timeout. Track placed_at; a tick() rule cancels PLACED orders older than, say, five minutes as Actor.PLATFORM, with a full refund. The state machine and refund policy already handle it. It's one more line in tick() and one more config field.
