Chapter 7: Traffic Management System

Type every file in order. Run each checkpoint before moving on. All commands run from the project folder (the one holding traffic_mgmt/ and tests/). Python 3.13, stdlib only.

Chapter 3 built one intersection. This chapter builds the city around it. There are many intersections now, joined by roads, each with its own controller. A central traffic management centre pushes timing plans out and receives telemetry back. Signals along a main road are coordinated so that a car at the right speed meets green after green. Ambulances get green lights ahead of them. Incidents close lanes. And when the radio link to a controller dies, which happens every day in a real city, that controller has to keep its junction safe on its own.

The Chapter 3 state machine is still the core. What changes is everything that talks to it.

1. The problem

A city runs a network of signalised intersections. Each one has two crossing movements, north-south (NS) and east-west (EW), and a controller that cycles green, yellow and all-red between them. How long each green lasts comes from a timing plan. There are three kinds: fixed-time, adaptive (it reads vehicle-count sensors), and time-of-day, which switches between plans by the clock.

A central Traffic Management Center (TMC) owns the city view. It pushes plans to controllers, coordinates a corridor into a green wave, preempts signals along an emergency vehicle's route, and records lane closures from incidents. Controllers send their phase changes and mode changes back as telemetry. If a controller stops hearing from the centre, it falls back to a safe local fixed-time plan. If its own hardware reports a fault, it drops to flashing red, an all-way stop, until a technician resets it.

Everything runs on a simulated clock that moves one second at a time. Same inputs, same outputs, every run.

2. Requirements

Clarifying questions to ask first. Practise asking these before you read the answers. The answers are the ones this chapter builds to.

  • How many movements per intersection? Two axes, NS and EW. Turn phases and pedestrian phases are out of scope (they come back as follow-ups).
  • Who decides green times: the centre or the controller? The centre chooses the plan. The controller runs it, one cycle at a time, and refuses any plan that would undercut its safety timings.
  • What happens when the centre is unreachable? The controller keeps running. After a configurable silence it switches to a local fixed-time plan. It never goes dark.
  • What does a hardware fault look like? A conflict monitor trips (for example, it sees two crossing greens). The controller flashes red at once and waits for a human reset.
  • Is emergency preemption allowed to skip yellow? No. Preemption can cut a green short, but yellow and all-red always run. A preempt that causes a crash is worse than a slow one.
  • How does the coordinated corridor run? Along the NS axis of each intersection on it, at one design speed and one common cycle length.
  • Real time or simulated? Simulated, tick by tick, with an injected clock. A deployment would drive the same step() from a one-second timer.
  • The 2026 angle: where does the centre live? Usually in the cloud, talking to roadside controllers over cellular links. That makes comms outages routine, not exceptional. Interviewers now expect you to design for the outage first. They also ask about connected-vehicle feeds (signal timing broadcast to cars), transit priority, and what stops a compromised centre from pushing a dangerous plan.

Functional

  • Model a road network: intersections joined by one-way links, each with a length, a lane count and the axis it arrives on.
  • Each intersection controller runs NS_GREEN → NS_YELLOW → ALL_RED → EW_GREEN → EW_YELLOW → ALL_RED, from an explicit transition table.
  • Green times come from a pluggable timing strategy: fixed-time, adaptive (from queue counts), time-of-day, or coordinated (for green waves).
  • A new plan takes effect at the next cycle boundary, never mid-phase.
  • The TMC designs green-wave offsets for a corridor from link lengths and a design speed.
  • The TMC dispatches an emergency vehicle along the shortest open route. Each signal on the route is preempted a lead time before the vehicle arrives and released shortly after it passes.
  • Incidents close lanes on a link. A full closure removes the link from routing; a partial closure lowers that approach's capacity, and the adaptive plan stretches its green to match.
  • Controllers publish phase, mode and cycle telemetry. The TMC subscribes.
  • Comms loss → local fixed-time fallback. Comms restored → back to the centre's plan. Hardware fault → flashing red until reset.

Non-functional

  • Deterministic: one shared, injectable clock; every loop runs in a fixed order.
  • Thread-safe: centre commands, sensor input and the simulation loop may run on different threads.
  • Safety is owned by the controller, not by the plan. No plan, command or preemption can skip a clearance interval.
  • Every duration and rate lives in a validated, frozen config dataclass.
  • Typed exception hierarchy; no bare except; logging in library code, print only in the demo.

4. Class diagram

Two classes carry the design, and the diagram marks both. The controller owns safety at one junction. The centre owns the city view. Everything else is a seam between them: plans flow down through a strategy, telemetry flows up through an uplink and a bus.

5. Key design decisions

  1. Safety lives in the controller, never in the plan. A timing plan answers one question: how many green seconds each axis gets this cycle. Yellow and all-red come from SafetyTimings, which only the controller reads. The controller also clamps any green below min_green. So a buggy plan, or a malicious one pushed from a compromised centre, can make traffic slow. It cannot make it unsafe. This is also what makes the plans substitutable (Liskov): any TimingStrategy is safe to plug in because none of them can touch the dangerous part.
  2. The transition table from Chapter 3 survives, with two new kinds of state. PREEMPT_NS and PREEMPT_EW are held greens for emergency vehicles. FLASHING_RED is the fault state. Entering a preempt goes through the table, so a cross-street green is cut via yellow and all-red first. Entering flash deliberately bypasses the table. A conflict monitor does not ask for permission.
  3. Plans change at the cycle boundary. apply_plan() only stores the plan. The controller asks for a new split when NS green begins. A mid-phase switch could shorten a green that drivers are already committed to; waiting a cycle costs at most a minute or two and removes a whole class of edge cases.
  4. Green waves are offsets on a shared beat. Every signal on a corridor runs the same cycle length. Each one's NS green starts later than its neighbour's by the travel time between them. CoordinatedPlan knows the beat. If a cycle starts off-beat (after boot, a plan change or a preemption), it stretches or squeezes that one cycle's EW green so the next cycle lands on the beat. Recovery is automatic and takes one cycle.
  5. The centre stores desired state and reconciles. set_plan() records what each controller should run, then tries to deliver it. If the link is down, the next heartbeat round after it comes back delivers it. The centre never has to remember to retry; reconciliation is the retry.
  6. Fail safe in both directions. The controller times out the centre (heartbeat) and falls back locally. It also times out its own preemption (max_preempt_hold), because a release command can be lost too. Neither side trusts the other to be there.
  7. Telemetry is store-and-forward. While the link is down, the Uplink buffers events in a bounded deque, oldest dropped first. When the link returns, the centre learns what happened during the outage, in order, instead of seeing a gap.
  8. One clock, one loop, one order. TrafficSimulation.run() advances the clock one second, steps every controller in id order, then steps the centre. That order is the determinism. Tests and the demo print identical output on every run.

Patterns, and why each earns its place. Strategy for timing plans: fixed, adaptive, time-of-day and coordinated vary independently of the state machine, and TimeOfDayPlan composes other strategies without the controller noticing. Observer for telemetry: the centre, a dashboard and a test recorder all subscribe the same way, and a failing listener cannot stop a signal. State machine as data for the phases, as in Chapter 3. A composition root (build_city) for wiring, so no class builds its own dependencies.

Lock order, stated once. TMC lock → controller lock → uplink lock → listener locks. The centre's on_event() takes only its own small _status_lock and never calls out. A controller publishing telemetry while the centre is commanding it therefore cannot deadlock. Say this out loud in an interview; it is the kind of sentence that separates "I added locks" from "I designed the locking".

Step 2 — Configuration

Goal: every number the system uses, in frozen, validated dataclasses.

Type traffic_mgmt/config.py:

"""Every tunable number in the system, in frozen, validated dataclasses.

No other module contains a literal duration, rate or limit. A traffic
engineer can audit the whole timing policy by reading this one file.
"""
from dataclasses import dataclass, field

from .errors import InvalidConfigError

SECONDS_PER_DAY = 86_400  # a unit conversion, not a tunable


def _require_positive_ints(obj: object, *names: str) -> None:
    for name in names:
        value = getattr(obj, name)
        if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, int) or value <= 0:
            raise InvalidConfigError(
                f"{type(obj).__name__}.{name} must be a positive int, got {value!r}"
            )


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class SafetyTimings:
    """Floors and clearances that no timing plan is allowed to undercut."""

    min_green: int = 7
    yellow: int = 3
    all_red: int = 2

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        _require_positive_ints(self, "min_green", "yellow", "all_red")

    @property
    def clearance(self) -> int:
        """Yellow plus all-red: the cost of handing the junction over once."""
        return self.yellow + self.all_red


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class AdaptiveTuning:
    """How the adaptive plan turns a queue count into green seconds."""

    base_green: int = 10
    seconds_per_vehicle: int = 2
    max_green: int = 60

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        _require_positive_ints(self, "base_green", "seconds_per_vehicle", "max_green")
        if self.base_green > self.max_green:
            raise InvalidConfigError("base_green cannot exceed max_green")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class DischargeConfig:
    """Saturation flow: one vehicle crosses the stop line every N green seconds."""

    seconds_per_departure: int = 2

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        _require_positive_ints(self, "seconds_per_departure")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class FailsafeConfig:
    """What a controller does when the centre goes quiet or hardware fails."""

    heartbeat_timeout: int = 15
    fallback_ns_green: int = 20
    fallback_ew_green: int = 20
    max_preempt_hold: int = 120
    telemetry_buffer: int = 100

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        _require_positive_ints(
            self,
            "heartbeat_timeout",
            "fallback_ns_green",
            "fallback_ew_green",
            "max_preempt_hold",
            "telemetry_buffer",
        )


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class ControllerConfig:
    """Everything one intersection controller needs to know."""

    safety: SafetyTimings = field(default_factory=SafetyTimings)
    discharge: DischargeConfig = field(default_factory=DischargeConfig)
    failsafe: FailsafeConfig = field(default_factory=FailsafeConfig)

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        for name in ("fallback_ns_green", "fallback_ew_green"):
            if getattr(self.failsafe, name) < self.safety.min_green:
                raise InvalidConfigError(f"failsafe.{name} is below safety.min_green")


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class TmcConfig:
    """The centre's own timing: heartbeats and emergency preemption windows."""

    heartbeat_interval: int = 5
    preempt_lead_time: int = 20
    preempt_release_after: int = 5

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        _require_positive_ints(
            self, "heartbeat_interval", "preempt_lead_time", "preempt_release_after"
        )

What to notice. isinstance(value, bool) is checked before int, because True is an int in Python and SafetyTimings(all_red=True) would otherwise sail through as one second. ControllerConfig also checks a cross-field rule: the fallback plan must respect the safety minimum. A fallback that fails validation at 3 a.m. during an outage is the worst possible time to find out.

Step 3 — The clock

Goal: one source of "now", in whole seconds, that only moves when the simulation says so.

Type traffic_mgmt/clock.py:

"""Injectable time. Domain code asks a Clock; it never reads the wall.

Time is a whole number of seconds. ManualClock starts at any second you
like (7 * 3600 is 07:00 on day zero) and moves only when the simulation
advances it, so every test and every demo run is exactly repeatable.
"""
import threading
import time
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod


class Clock(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def now(self) -> int:
        """Current time in whole seconds."""


class SystemClock(Clock):
    """Wall-clock seconds since the Unix epoch (UTC) for a real deployment."""

    def now(self) -> int:
        return int(time.time())


class ManualClock(Clock):
    """A clock that moves only when told to. Safe to share between threads."""

    def __init__(self, start: int = 0) -> None:
        if start < 0:
            raise ValueError("start must be >= 0")
        self._now = start
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _now

    def now(self) -> int:
        with self._lock:
            return self._now

    def advance(self, seconds: int) -> None:
        if seconds < 0:
            raise ValueError("time only moves forward")
        with self._lock:
            self._now += seconds

What to notice. Chapter 3 passed seconds into tick(). Here many controllers and the centre must agree on the time, so the clock becomes a shared object. ManualClock has a lock because the simulation thread advances it while command threads read it.

Step 4 — The vocabulary

Goal: axes, phases, modes, and the GreenSplit value object.

Type traffic_mgmt/models.py:

"""The vocabulary: axes, phases, control modes, and the green split."""
from dataclasses import dataclass
from enum import Enum

from .errors import InvalidPlanError


class Axis(Enum):
    """The two movements at a two-road intersection."""

    NS = "ns"
    EW = "ew"

    @property
    def other(self) -> "Axis":
        return Axis.EW if self is Axis.NS else Axis.NS


class Phase(Enum):
    NS_GREEN = "ns_green"
    NS_YELLOW = "ns_yellow"
    ALL_RED = "all_red"
    EW_GREEN = "ew_green"
    EW_YELLOW = "ew_yellow"
    PREEMPT_NS = "preempt_ns"  # held green for an emergency vehicle
    PREEMPT_EW = "preempt_ew"
    FLASHING_RED = "flashing_red"  # fault flash: every approach is an all-way stop


GREEN: dict[Axis, Phase] = {Axis.NS: Phase.NS_GREEN, Axis.EW: Phase.EW_GREEN}
YELLOW: dict[Axis, Phase] = {Axis.NS: Phase.NS_YELLOW, Axis.EW: Phase.EW_YELLOW}
PREEMPT: dict[Axis, Phase] = {Axis.NS: Phase.PREEMPT_NS, Axis.EW: Phase.PREEMPT_EW}


def moving_axis(phase: Phase) -> Axis | None:
    """The axis whose traffic may enter the junction in this phase, if any."""
    for axis in Axis:
        if phase in (GREEN[axis], PREEMPT[axis]):
            return axis
    return None


class ControlMode(Enum):
    CENTRAL = "central"  # running the plan the centre pushed
    LOCAL_FALLBACK = "local_fallback"  # centre silent: safe local fixed-time plan
    FLASHING = "flashing"  # hardware fault: flash until a technician resets


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class GreenSplit:
    """Green seconds for each axis in one cycle, and the plan that chose them."""

    ns: int
    ew: int
    plan: str

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        for name in ("ns", "ew"):
            value = getattr(self, name)
            if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, int) or value <= 0:
                raise InvalidPlanError(f"green for {name} must be a positive int, got {value!r}")

    def green_for(self, axis: Axis) -> int:
        return self.ns if axis is Axis.NS else self.ew

What to notice. The GREEN, YELLOW and PREEMPT maps let the controller write GREEN[axis] instead of an if-chain per axis. moving_axis() answers the one safety question that matters, which axis may enter the junction right now. A held preempt green counts. A yellow does not.

Step 5 — Sensors and queues

Goal: a small, exact model of the road surface. Vehicles arrive, queue, and leave during green at the saturation-flow rate.

Type traffic_mgmt/sensors.py:

"""Vehicle-count sensors and the queues they measure.

ApproachQueues is the simulated road surface at one intersection: vehicles
arrive on an approach, wait, and leave during that approach's green at the
saturation-flow rate. count() is what the loop detector reports.

Fractions keep the discharge arithmetic exact, so a lane closure that
halves capacity halves departures with no float dust.
"""
import threading
from fractions import Fraction

from .config import DischargeConfig
from .models import Axis

FULL_CAPACITY = Fraction(1)


class ApproachQueues:
    """Queue length and capacity per axis at one intersection. Thread-safe."""

    def __init__(self, discharge: DischargeConfig) -> None:
        self._discharge = discharge
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards the three dicts below
        self._queue: dict[Axis, int] = {axis: 0 for axis in Axis}
        self._credit: dict[Axis, Fraction] = {axis: Fraction(0) for axis in Axis}
        self._capacity: dict[Axis, Fraction] = {axis: FULL_CAPACITY for axis in Axis}

    def arrive(self, axis: Axis, vehicles: int) -> None:
        if isinstance(vehicles, bool) or not isinstance(vehicles, int) or vehicles < 0:
            raise ValueError(f"vehicles must be a non-negative int, got {vehicles!r}")
        with self._lock:
            self._queue[axis] += vehicles

    def discharge(self, axis: Axis, green_seconds: int = 1) -> int:
        """Let vehicles leave for green_seconds of green. Returns how many left."""
        with self._lock:
            rate = self._capacity[axis] / self._discharge.seconds_per_departure
            self._credit[axis] += rate * green_seconds
            leaving = min(self._queue[axis], int(self._credit[axis]))
            self._queue[axis] -= leaving
            self._credit[axis] -= leaving
            if self._queue[axis] == 0:
                self._credit[axis] = Fraction(0)  # an empty road banks no green
            return leaving

    def count(self, axis: Axis) -> int:
        """The detector reading: vehicles currently queued on this approach."""
        with self._lock:
            return self._queue[axis]

    def set_capacity(self, axis: Axis, capacity: Fraction) -> None:
        """Share of normal saturation flow still available (lane closures)."""
        if not 0 < capacity <= 1:
            raise ValueError(f"capacity must be in (0, 1], got {capacity}")
        with self._lock:
            self._capacity[axis] = Fraction(capacity)

    def capacity(self, axis: Axis) -> Fraction:
        with self._lock:
            return self._capacity[axis]

What to notice. The checkpoint below discharges ten queued cars. Six seconds of green at one car per two seconds lets 3 through, leaving 7. Then a lane closure halves capacity. Another six seconds earns only 1.5 departures: 1 car leaves and the half-car of credit carries over. Fraction keeps that arithmetic exact. With floats, the credit would drift and a test would flake one day in a thousand.

Checkpoint:

Step 6 — Telemetry events and the bus

Goal: the Observer half of the design. Controllers publish events; the centre and anyone else subscribe.

Type traffic_mgmt/events.py:

"""Telemetry events and the bus that carries them (Observer).

Controllers publish; the TrafficManagementCenter, dashboards and tests
subscribe. Publishers never know who is listening.
"""
import logging
import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from dataclasses import dataclass

from .models import ControlMode, GreenSplit, Phase

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class TelemetryEvent:
    intersection_id: str
    at: int


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class PhaseChanged(TelemetryEvent):
    phase: Phase


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class ModeChanged(TelemetryEvent):
    mode: ControlMode
    reason: str


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class CycleStarted(TelemetryEvent):
    split: GreenSplit
    queue_ns: int
    queue_ew: int


class TelemetryListener(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def on_event(self, event: TelemetryEvent) -> None:
        """Called synchronously on the publisher's thread. Keep it short and
        never call back into a controller from here."""


class TelemetrySink(ABC):
    """Where a controller sends its events. The controller knows nothing more."""

    @abstractmethod
    def send(self, event: TelemetryEvent) -> None:
        ...


class EventBus(TelemetrySink):
    """Fan-out to every subscriber. A failing listener is logged, not fatal."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _listeners
        self._listeners: list[TelemetryListener] = []

    def subscribe(self, listener: TelemetryListener) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._listeners.append(listener)

    def send(self, event: TelemetryEvent) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            listeners = list(self._listeners)  # deliver outside the lock
        for listener in listeners:
            try:
                listener.on_event(event)
            except Exception:  # one bad dashboard must not stop the signals
                log.exception("listener %r failed on %r", listener, event)


class RecordingListener(TelemetryListener):
    """Keeps every event it sees. Handy in demos and tests."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _events
        self._events: list[TelemetryEvent] = []

    def on_event(self, event: TelemetryEvent) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._events.append(event)

    def events(self) -> list[TelemetryEvent]:
        with self._lock:
            return list(self._events)

What to notice. EventBus.send() copies the listener list under the lock, then delivers outside it. A listener that subscribes another listener from inside on_event() can't deadlock the bus. The one except Exception is deliberate and logged: a broken dashboard must not stop traffic lights. KeyboardInterrupt still propagates.

Step 7 — Comms and the store-and-forward uplink

Goal: model the link between centre and controller, and make telemetry survive an outage.

Type traffic_mgmt/comms.py:

"""The radio/fibre link between the centre and each controller.

CommsNetwork answers one question: is this controller reachable right now?
InMemoryComms lets tests and demos cut and restore links on purpose.

Uplink is a controller's outbound telemetry path. While the link is down it
stores events in a bounded buffer (oldest dropped first) and forwards them,
in order, as soon as the link is back. Store-and-forward, so the centre
learns what happened during the outage instead of a blank.
"""
import logging
import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from collections import deque

from .events import TelemetryEvent, TelemetrySink

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)


class CommsNetwork(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def is_up(self, intersection_id: str) -> bool:
        ...


class InMemoryComms(CommsNetwork):
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _down
        self._down: set[str] = set()

    def is_up(self, intersection_id: str) -> bool:
        with self._lock:
            return intersection_id not in self._down

    def sever(self, intersection_id: str) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._down.add(intersection_id)
        log.warning("comms to %s severed", intersection_id)

    def restore(self, intersection_id: str) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._down.discard(intersection_id)
        log.info("comms to %s restored", intersection_id)


class Uplink(TelemetrySink):
    """Store-and-forward telemetry for one controller."""

    def __init__(
        self,
        intersection_id: str,
        comms: CommsNetwork,
        downstream: TelemetrySink,
        capacity: int,
    ) -> None:
        if capacity <= 0:
            raise ValueError("capacity must be positive")
        self._id = intersection_id
        self._comms = comms
        self._downstream = downstream
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _buffer and _dropped; held while forwarding
        self._buffer: deque[TelemetryEvent] = deque(maxlen=capacity)
        self._dropped = 0

    def send(self, event: TelemetryEvent) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            if not self._comms.is_up(self._id):
                if len(self._buffer) == self._buffer.maxlen:
                    self._dropped += 1
                self._buffer.append(event)
                return
            # Held while forwarding so two threads cannot reorder the stream.
            while self._buffer:
                self._downstream.send(self._buffer.popleft())
            self._downstream.send(event)

    @property
    def buffered(self) -> int:
        with self._lock:
            return len(self._buffer)

    @property
    def dropped(self) -> int:
        with self._lock:
            return self._dropped

What to notice. Uplink.send() holds its lock while forwarding. That is unusual (Step 6 delivered outside its lock) and it is on purpose. Two threads flushing the same buffer could otherwise interleave and deliver events out of order. The lock order in Section 5 is what makes holding it safe. In the checkpoint, the comms to A severed line comes from logging's last-resort handler, which prints WARNING and above when nothing else is configured.

Step 8 — Timing plans

Goal: the Strategy family. Four plans, one method.

Type traffic_mgmt/timing.py:

"""Timing plans (Strategy): how long each axis stays green in the next cycle.

The controller asks its plan once per cycle, at the instant north-south
green begins. A plan sees a CycleContext (time, detector counts, capacity)
and answers with a GreenSplit. It never touches yellow or all-red: those
belong to SafetyTimings and the controller, so no plan can make a junction
unsafe. That is what lets any plan substitute for any other.
"""
import math
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from collections.abc import Sequence
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction

from .config import SECONDS_PER_DAY, AdaptiveTuning, SafetyTimings
from .errors import InvalidPlanError
from .models import Axis, GreenSplit


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class CycleContext:
    now: int
    queue_ns: int
    queue_ew: int
    capacity_ns: Fraction = Fraction(1)
    capacity_ew: Fraction = Fraction(1)

    def queue(self, axis: Axis) -> int:
        return self.queue_ns if axis is Axis.NS else self.queue_ew

    def capacity(self, axis: Axis) -> Fraction:
        return self.capacity_ns if axis is Axis.NS else self.capacity_ew


class TimingStrategy(ABC):
    @property
    @abstractmethod
    def name(self) -> str:
        ...

    @abstractmethod
    def split(self, ctx: CycleContext) -> GreenSplit:
        """Green seconds for each axis in the cycle starting at ctx.now."""


class FixedTimePlan(TimingStrategy):
    """The same split every cycle. Also the safe local fallback."""

    def __init__(self, ns_green: int, ew_green: int, name: str = "fixed") -> None:
        self._split = GreenSplit(ns_green, ew_green, name)  # validates
        self._name = name

    @property
    def name(self) -> str:
        return self._name

    def split(self, ctx: CycleContext) -> GreenSplit:
        return self._split


class AdaptivePlan(TimingStrategy):
    """Green grows with the queue: base + seconds_per_vehicle * queue / capacity.

    Dividing by capacity means a lane closure (capacity 1/2) asks for twice
    the green to clear the same queue. The result is capped at max_green so
    one approach can never starve the other.
    """

    def __init__(self, tuning: AdaptiveTuning, name: str = "adaptive") -> None:
        self._tuning = tuning
        self._name = name

    @property
    def name(self) -> str:
        return self._name

    def _green(self, ctx: CycleContext, axis: Axis) -> int:
        t = self._tuning
        needed = t.seconds_per_vehicle * ctx.queue(axis) / ctx.capacity(axis)
        return min(t.max_green, t.base_green + math.ceil(needed))

    def split(self, ctx: CycleContext) -> GreenSplit:
        return GreenSplit(self._green(ctx, Axis.NS), self._green(ctx, Axis.EW), self._name)


class TimeOfDayPlan(TimingStrategy):
    """Picks a sub-plan by time of day: e.g. peak adaptive, night fixed.

    entries are (start_second_of_day, plan), sorted by start. Before the first
    start, the last entry is still in force from the previous evening.
    """

    def __init__(self, entries: Sequence[tuple[int, TimingStrategy]], name: str = "time-of-day") -> None:
        if not entries:
            raise InvalidPlanError("a time-of-day plan needs at least one entry")
        starts = [start for start, _ in entries]
        if starts != sorted(set(starts)):
            raise InvalidPlanError("entry start times must be unique and ascending")
        if starts[0] < 0 or starts[-1] >= SECONDS_PER_DAY:
            raise InvalidPlanError("entry start times must fall within one day")
        self._entries = tuple(entries)
        self._name = name

    @property
    def name(self) -> str:
        return self._name

    def plan_at(self, now: int) -> TimingStrategy:
        second_of_day = now % SECONDS_PER_DAY
        active = self._entries[-1][1]  # wraps from yesterday
        for start, plan in self._entries:
            if start <= second_of_day:
                active = plan
        return active

    def split(self, ctx: CycleContext) -> GreenSplit:
        return self.plan_at(ctx.now).split(ctx)


class CoordinatedPlan(TimingStrategy):
    """A fixed cycle locked to a city-wide time base: the green-wave building block.

    North-south green starts whenever (now - offset) % cycle_length == 0.
    If a cycle starts off-beat (power-up, plan change, after a preemption),
    this plan stretches or shrinks the east-west green of that one cycle so
    the next one lands exactly on the beat. Then every later cycle is normal.
    """

    def __init__(
        self,
        cycle_length: int,
        offset: int,
        ns_green: int,
        safety: SafetyTimings,
        name: str = "coordinated",
    ) -> None:
        self._overhead = 2 * safety.clearance  # two handovers per cycle
        self._min_green = safety.min_green
        if ns_green < safety.min_green:
            raise InvalidPlanError("ns_green is below the safety minimum")
        if cycle_length < ns_green + safety.min_green + self._overhead:
            raise InvalidPlanError(
                f"cycle {cycle_length}s cannot fit ns_green={ns_green}, a minimum "
                f"cross green and {self._overhead}s of clearance"
            )
        if not 0 <= offset < cycle_length:
            raise InvalidPlanError("offset must be in [0, cycle_length)")
        self.cycle_length = cycle_length
        self.offset = offset
        self.ns_green = ns_green
        self._name = name

    @property
    def name(self) -> str:
        return self._name

    def split(self, ctx: CycleContext) -> GreenSplit:
        drift = (ctx.now - self.offset) % self.cycle_length
        length = self.cycle_length - drift  # seconds until the next beat
        if length < self.ns_green + self._min_green + self._overhead:
            length += self.cycle_length  # too short to be safe: aim for the beat after
        ew_green = length - self.ns_green - self._overhead
        return GreenSplit(self.ns_green, ew_green, self._name)

Step 9 — The intersection controller

Goal: the Chapter 3 state machine, grown up. It now has a plan, a heartbeat, preemption and a fault state.

Type traffic_mgmt/controller.py:

"""One intersection's signal controller: a state machine with three masters.

1. Its timing plan (Strategy) sets the green split, once per cycle.
2. The centre sends heartbeats, plans and preemption calls. Silence for
   longer than heartbeat_timeout drops the controller to its local
   fixed-time fallback plan. The next heartbeat brings it back.
3. Safety outranks both. Every phase change goes through TRANSITIONS,
   yellow and all-red are never skipped, and a hardware fault drops
   straight into FLASHING_RED, the one move that needs no permission.

The controller never reads a wall clock. The simulation advances the
shared Clock by one second and then calls step() exactly once.
"""
import logging
import threading
from fractions import Fraction

from .clock import Clock
from .config import ControllerConfig
from .errors import IllegalTransitionError, PreemptionConflictError, SignalFaultError
from .events import CycleStarted, ModeChanged, PhaseChanged, TelemetrySink
from .models import GREEN, PREEMPT, YELLOW, Axis, ControlMode, GreenSplit, Phase
from .sensors import ApproachQueues
from .timing import CycleContext, FixedTimePlan, TimingStrategy

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)

TRANSITIONS: dict[Phase, frozenset[Phase]] = {
    Phase.ALL_RED: frozenset(
        {Phase.NS_GREEN, Phase.EW_GREEN, Phase.PREEMPT_NS, Phase.PREEMPT_EW}
    ),
    Phase.NS_GREEN: frozenset({Phase.NS_YELLOW, Phase.PREEMPT_NS}),
    Phase.NS_YELLOW: frozenset({Phase.ALL_RED}),
    Phase.EW_GREEN: frozenset({Phase.EW_YELLOW, Phase.PREEMPT_EW}),
    Phase.EW_YELLOW: frozenset({Phase.ALL_RED}),
    Phase.PREEMPT_NS: frozenset({Phase.NS_YELLOW}),
    Phase.PREEMPT_EW: frozenset({Phase.EW_YELLOW}),
    Phase.FLASHING_RED: frozenset({Phase.ALL_RED}),
}
# Not in the table on purpose: any phase -> FLASHING_RED. A conflict monitor
# that detects a fault does not ask the state machine for permission.

_HELD = frozenset({Phase.PREEMPT_NS, Phase.PREEMPT_EW, Phase.FLASHING_RED})


class IntersectionController:
    """Thread-safe: every public method takes self._lock (an RLock)."""

    def __init__(
        self,
        intersection_id: str,
        clock: Clock,
        queues: ApproachQueues,
        telemetry: TelemetrySink,
        config: ControllerConfig = ControllerConfig(),
        fallback: TimingStrategy | None = None,
    ) -> None:
        if not intersection_id:
            raise ValueError("intersection_id must be non-empty")
        self.intersection_id = intersection_id
        self._clock = clock
        self._queues = queues
        self._telemetry = telemetry
        self._safety = config.safety
        self._failsafe = config.failsafe
        self._fallback = fallback or FixedTimePlan(
            config.failsafe.fallback_ns_green, config.failsafe.fallback_ew_green, "local-fixed"
        )
        # self._lock guards every attribute below. Lock order across the
        # system: TMC lock -> controller lock -> uplink lock -> listener locks.
        self._lock = threading.RLock()
        self._phase = Phase.ALL_RED  # boot into clearance, never into a green
        self._elapsed = 0
        self._duration = self._safety.all_red
        self._next_axis = Axis.NS
        self._split: GreenSplit | None = None
        self._central_plan: TimingStrategy | None = None
        self._mode = ControlMode.LOCAL_FALLBACK  # until the centre says hello
        self._last_heartbeat: int | None = None
        self._preempt_axis: Axis | None = None
        self._preempt_holders: set[str] = set()

    # ------------------------------------------------------------ queries
    @property
    def phase(self) -> Phase:
        with self._lock:
            return self._phase

    @property
    def mode(self) -> ControlMode:
        with self._lock:
            return self._mode

    @property
    def preempted_by(self) -> frozenset[str]:
        with self._lock:
            return frozenset(self._preempt_holders)

    @property
    def heartbeat_timeout(self) -> int:
        return self._failsafe.heartbeat_timeout

    # ------------------------------------------------------- time passing
    def step(self) -> None:
        """Account for the one second that just elapsed on the shared clock."""
        with self._lock:
            if self._phase is Phase.FLASHING_RED:
                return
            self._check_heartbeat()
            for axis in Axis:
                if self._phase in (GREEN[axis], PREEMPT[axis]):
                    self._queues.discharge(axis)
            self._elapsed += 1
            if self._phase in _HELD:
                if self._elapsed >= self._failsafe.max_preempt_hold:
                    log.warning("%s: preemption held %ss, auto-releasing",
                                self.intersection_id, self._elapsed)
                    self._end_preemption()
                return
            if self._elapsed >= self._duration:
                self._advance()

    # ----------------------------------------------------- centre commands
    def heartbeat(self) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._last_heartbeat = self._clock.now()
            if self._mode is ControlMode.LOCAL_FALLBACK:
                self._set_mode(ControlMode.CENTRAL, "heartbeat received")

    def apply_plan(self, plan: TimingStrategy) -> None:
        """Store the centre's plan. It takes effect at the next cycle start."""
        with self._lock:
            self._central_plan = plan
            log.info("%s: plan %r queued for next cycle", self.intersection_id, plan.name)

    def preempt(self, axis: Axis, vehicle_id: str) -> None:
        """Give `axis` a held green for an emergency vehicle, safely."""
        with self._lock:
            if self._phase is Phase.FLASHING_RED:
                raise SignalFaultError(f"{self.intersection_id} is in fault flash")
            if self._preempt_axis not in (None, axis):
                raise PreemptionConflictError(
                    f"{self.intersection_id} already preempted for {self._preempt_axis.name}"
                )
            self._preempt_holders.add(vehicle_id)
            self._preempt_axis = axis
            if self._phase is GREEN[axis]:
                self._enter(PREEMPT[axis])  # already green: just hold it
            elif self._phase is GREEN[axis.other]:
                self._enter(YELLOW[axis.other])  # cut the cross green, keep clearance
            # Yellow or all-red: let clearance finish; _advance routes to PREEMPT.

    def release(self, vehicle_id: str) -> bool:
        """Drop one vehicle's hold. Returns False if it held nothing here."""
        with self._lock:
            if vehicle_id not in self._preempt_holders:
                return False
            self._preempt_holders.discard(vehicle_id)
            if not self._preempt_holders:
                self._end_preemption()
            return True

    def set_capacity(self, axis: Axis, capacity: Fraction) -> None:
        """Record a lane closure so adaptive plans can stretch that green."""
        with self._lock:
            self._queues.set_capacity(axis, capacity)

    # ------------------------------------------------------ fault handling
    def report_fault(self, reason: str) -> None:
        """Conflict monitor tripped: flash immediately, drop everything else."""
        with self._lock:
            self._preempt_holders.clear()
            self._preempt_axis = None
            self._enter(Phase.FLASHING_RED, force=True)
            self._set_mode(ControlMode.FLASHING, reason)

    def reset_fault(self) -> None:
        """Technician reset: clearance first, then a fresh cycle."""
        with self._lock:
            if self._phase is not Phase.FLASHING_RED:
                raise IllegalTransitionError(f"{self.intersection_id} is not flashing")
            self._next_axis = Axis.NS
            self._split = None
            self._enter(Phase.ALL_RED)
            self._set_mode(
                ControlMode.CENTRAL if self._heartbeat_fresh() else ControlMode.LOCAL_FALLBACK,
                "fault reset",
            )

    # ------------------------------------------------------------ internals
    def _heartbeat_fresh(self) -> bool:
        if self._last_heartbeat is None:
            return False
        return self._clock.now() - self._last_heartbeat <= self._failsafe.heartbeat_timeout

    def _check_heartbeat(self) -> None:
        if self._mode is ControlMode.CENTRAL and not self._heartbeat_fresh():
            self._set_mode(ControlMode.LOCAL_FALLBACK, "heartbeat timeout")

    def _set_mode(self, mode: ControlMode, reason: str) -> None:
        if mode is self._mode:
            return
        self._mode = mode
        level = logging.INFO if mode is ControlMode.CENTRAL else logging.WARNING
        log.log(level, "%s: mode -> %s (%s)", self.intersection_id, mode.name, reason)
        self._telemetry.send(ModeChanged(self.intersection_id, self._clock.now(), mode, reason))

    def _end_preemption(self) -> None:
        axis, self._preempt_axis = self._preempt_axis, None
        self._preempt_holders.clear()
        if axis is not None and self._phase is PREEMPT[axis]:
            self._enter(YELLOW[axis])

    def _advance(self) -> None:
        phase = self._phase
        if phase in (Phase.NS_GREEN, Phase.EW_GREEN):
            axis = Axis.NS if phase is Phase.NS_GREEN else Axis.EW
            self._enter(YELLOW[axis])
        elif phase in (Phase.NS_YELLOW, Phase.EW_YELLOW):
            axis = Axis.NS if phase is Phase.NS_YELLOW else Axis.EW
            self._next_axis = axis.other
            self._enter(Phase.ALL_RED)
        elif phase is Phase.ALL_RED:
            if self._preempt_axis is not None:
                self._enter(PREEMPT[self._preempt_axis])
            else:
                if self._next_axis is Axis.NS or self._split is None:
                    self._split = self._plan_cycle()
                self._enter(GREEN[self._next_axis])

    def _active_plan(self) -> TimingStrategy:
        if self._mode is ControlMode.CENTRAL and self._central_plan is not None:
            return self._central_plan
        return self._fallback

    def _plan_cycle(self) -> GreenSplit:
        plan = self._active_plan()
        ctx = CycleContext(
            now=self._clock.now(),
            queue_ns=self._queues.count(Axis.NS),
            queue_ew=self._queues.count(Axis.EW),
            capacity_ns=self._queues.capacity(Axis.NS),
            capacity_ew=self._queues.capacity(Axis.EW),
        )
        raw = plan.split(ctx)
        floor = self._safety.min_green
        split = GreenSplit(max(raw.ns, floor), max(raw.ew, floor), raw.plan)
        if split != raw:
            log.warning("%s: plan %r undercut min_green; clamped", self.intersection_id, raw.plan)
        self._telemetry.send(
            CycleStarted(self.intersection_id, ctx.now, split, ctx.queue_ns, ctx.queue_ew)
        )
        return split

    def _duration_of(self, phase: Phase) -> int:
        if phase in (Phase.NS_YELLOW, Phase.EW_YELLOW):
            return self._safety.yellow
        if phase is Phase.ALL_RED:
            return self._safety.all_red
        if phase in (Phase.NS_GREEN, Phase.EW_GREEN) and self._split is not None:
            return self._split.green_for(Axis.NS if phase is Phase.NS_GREEN else Axis.EW)
        return 0  # held phases have no duration; step() never times them out

    def _enter(self, phase: Phase, *, force: bool = False) -> None:
        if not force and phase not in TRANSITIONS[self._phase]:
            raise IllegalTransitionError(f"{self._phase.name} -> {phase.name}")
        self._phase = phase
        self._elapsed = 0
        self._duration = self._duration_of(phase)
        log.debug("%s: %s", self.intersection_id, phase.name)
        self._telemetry.send(PhaseChanged(self.intersection_id, self._clock.now(), phase))

What to notice. Four things. First, the controller boots into ALL_RED, not a green, and in LOCAL_FALLBACK, not CENTRAL. It earns central control by hearing a heartbeat. Second, preempt() never jumps to a held green from the cross street. It enters the cross street's yellow, and _advance() routes the following all-red into the preempt. Third, step() checks the heartbeat before it times the phase, so a timeout and a cycle boundary in the same second resolve in a fixed order. Fourth, _plan_cycle() clamps whatever the plan returns. That clamp is the safety guarantee from Section 5, in two lines.

Type traffic_mgmt/network.py:

"""The road network: intersections joined by one-way links.

A Link runs from one intersection to the next and arrives at the downstream
intersection on one axis (its approach). Lane closures live here, so the
router and the incident handler read the same truth.
"""
import heapq
import threading
from dataclasses import dataclass
from fractions import Fraction

from .errors import NoRouteError, UnknownIntersectionError, UnknownLinkError
from .models import Axis


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Link:
    link_id: str
    from_id: str
    to_id: str
    length_m: int
    approach: Axis  # the axis this traffic uses at to_id
    lanes: int = 2

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if self.length_m <= 0 or self.lanes <= 0:
            raise ValueError(f"link {self.link_id}: length and lanes must be positive")
        if self.from_id == self.to_id:
            raise ValueError(f"link {self.link_id} loops onto itself")


class RoadNetwork:
    """Graph of intersections and links. Thread-safe."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards the three collections below
        self._intersections: set[str] = set()
        self._links: dict[str, Link] = {}
        self._lanes_closed: dict[str, int] = {}

    def add_intersection(self, intersection_id: str) -> None:
        if not intersection_id:
            raise ValueError("intersection_id must be non-empty")
        with self._lock:
            self._intersections.add(intersection_id)

    def add_link(self, link: Link) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            for end in (link.from_id, link.to_id):
                if end not in self._intersections:
                    raise UnknownIntersectionError(end)
            if link.link_id in self._links:
                raise ValueError(f"duplicate link {link.link_id}")
            self._links[link.link_id] = link
            self._lanes_closed[link.link_id] = 0

    def link(self, link_id: str) -> Link:
        with self._lock:
            return self._get(link_id)

    def intersections(self) -> list[str]:
        with self._lock:
            return sorted(self._intersections)

    def set_lanes_closed(self, link_id: str, lanes_closed: int) -> Fraction:
        """Record an incident. Returns the share of capacity still open."""
        with self._lock:
            link = self._get(link_id)
            if not 0 <= lanes_closed <= link.lanes:
                raise ValueError(f"lanes_closed must be in [0, {link.lanes}]")
            self._lanes_closed[link_id] = lanes_closed
            return Fraction(link.lanes - lanes_closed, link.lanes)

    def is_open(self, link_id: str) -> bool:
        with self._lock:
            link = self._get(link_id)
            return self._lanes_closed[link_id] < link.lanes

    def shortest_route(self, origin: str, destination: str) -> list[Link]:
        """Dijkstra by length over open links. Ties break by link id."""
        with self._lock:
            for end in (origin, destination):
                if end not in self._intersections:
                    raise UnknownIntersectionError(end)
            outgoing: dict[str, list[Link]] = {}
            for link in sorted(self._links.values(), key=lambda lk: lk.link_id):
                if self._lanes_closed[link.link_id] < link.lanes:
                    outgoing.setdefault(link.from_id, []).append(link)
        best: dict[str, int] = {origin: 0}
        came_by: dict[str, Link] = {}
        frontier: list[tuple[int, str]] = [(0, origin)]
        while frontier:
            dist, node = heapq.heappop(frontier)
            if node == destination:
                break
            if dist > best[node]:
                continue
            for link in outgoing.get(node, []):
                candidate = dist + link.length_m
                if candidate < best.get(link.to_id, candidate + 1):
                    best[link.to_id] = candidate
                    came_by[link.to_id] = link
                    heapq.heappush(frontier, (candidate, link.to_id))
        if destination not in best:
            raise NoRouteError(f"no open route {origin} -> {destination}")
        route: list[Link] = []
        node = destination
        while node != origin:
            route.append(came_by[node])
            node = came_by[node].from_id
        return route[::-1]

    def _get(self, link_id: str) -> Link:
        try:
            return self._links[link_id]
        except KeyError:
            raise UnknownLinkError(link_id) from None

What to notice. shortest_route() copies the open links under the lock and runs Dijkstra outside it. A long route search never blocks an incident report. Links are sorted by id before the search, so two equal-length routes always resolve the same way, and the tests can pin the answer.

Step 11 — The green-wave designer

Goal: turn a corridor of links into one CoordinatedPlan per intersection.

Type traffic_mgmt/coordination.py:

"""Green-wave design for a corridor of intersections.

Every signal on the corridor runs the same cycle length. Each one's
north-south green starts later than its upstream neighbour's by the travel
time between them. A car that leaves on green at design speed then meets
green at every downstream signal.
"""
from collections.abc import Sequence

from .config import SafetyTimings
from .errors import InvalidPlanError
from .models import Axis
from .network import RoadNetwork
from .timing import CoordinatedPlan


class GreenWaveDesigner:
    def __init__(self, network: RoadNetwork, safety: SafetyTimings) -> None:
        self._network = network
        self._safety = safety

    def design(
        self,
        link_ids: Sequence[str],
        speed_mps: float,
        cycle_length: int,
        ns_green: int,
    ) -> dict[str, CoordinatedPlan]:
        """One CoordinatedPlan per intersection, keyed by id, in corridor order."""
        if not link_ids:
            raise InvalidPlanError("a corridor needs at least one link")
        if speed_mps <= 0:
            raise InvalidPlanError("design speed must be positive")
        links = [self._network.link(link_id) for link_id in link_ids]
        for prev, nxt in zip(links, links[1:]):
            if prev.to_id != nxt.from_id:
                raise InvalidPlanError(f"{prev.link_id} does not lead into {nxt.link_id}")
        for link in links:
            if link.approach is not Axis.NS:
                raise InvalidPlanError(f"{link.link_id}: corridors run on the NS axis")
        offsets = {links[0].from_id: 0}
        for link in links:
            travel = round(link.length_m / speed_mps)
            offsets[link.to_id] = (offsets[link.from_id] + travel) % cycle_length
        return {
            iid: CoordinatedPlan(cycle_length, offset, ns_green, self._safety, f"wave@{offset}s")
            for iid, offset in offsets.items()
        }

What to notice. The offsets are cumulative travel times, wrapped by the cycle length. A-B is 300 m at 15 m/s, so B's green starts 20 s after A's. B-C is 450 m, another 30 s, so C sits at 50 s. The designer refuses a corridor that isn't contiguous or that isn't on the NS axis; a wave across a gap is not a wave.

Step 12 — The Traffic Management Center

Goal: the centre. Desired plans, heartbeat rounds, emergency runs and incidents, plus a telemetry listener.

Type traffic_mgmt/tmc.py:

"""The TrafficManagementCenter: plans out, heartbeats out, telemetry in.

The centre holds *desired* state (which plan each controller should run)
and reconciles it on every heartbeat round. A plan set while a link is down
is not lost: it is delivered on the first round after the link returns.

Emergency preemption runs along a route. Each intersection on the route is
preempted preempt_lead_time seconds before the vehicle's ETA and released
preempt_release_after seconds after it. A leg that cannot be preempted yet
(link down, conflicting preemption, fault flash) is retried every second.

Lock order: self._lock -> controller lock -> uplink lock -> self._status_lock.
on_event() takes only _status_lock and never calls out, so a controller
publishing telemetry can never deadlock against the centre.
"""
import logging
import math
import threading
from collections.abc import Callable, Mapping
from dataclasses import dataclass
from enum import Enum

from .clock import Clock
from .comms import CommsNetwork
from .config import TmcConfig
from .controller import IntersectionController
from .errors import (
    CommunicationLostError,
    EmergencyDispatchError,
    InvalidConfigError,
    TrafficError,
    UnknownIntersectionError,
)
from .events import ModeChanged, PhaseChanged, TelemetryEvent, TelemetryListener
from .models import Axis, ControlMode, Phase
from .network import RoadNetwork
from .timing import TimingStrategy

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)


class LegState(Enum):
    PENDING = "pending"
    HELD = "held"
    RELEASED = "released"
    MISSED = "missed"  # the vehicle passed before we could ever preempt


@dataclass
class _Leg:
    intersection_id: str
    approach: Axis
    eta: int
    state: LegState = LegState.PENDING


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class IntersectionStatus:
    phase: Phase | None
    mode: ControlMode | None
    last_heard: int | None


class TrafficManagementCenter(TelemetryListener):
    def __init__(
        self,
        network: RoadNetwork,
        comms: CommsNetwork,
        clock: Clock,
        config: TmcConfig = TmcConfig(),
    ) -> None:
        self._network = network
        self._comms = comms
        self._clock = clock
        self._config = config
        self._lock = threading.RLock()  # guards controllers, plans, runs, round time
        self._controllers: dict[str, IntersectionController] = {}
        self._desired: dict[str, TimingStrategy] = {}
        self._undelivered: set[str] = set()
        self._runs: dict[str, list[_Leg]] = {}
        self._last_round: int | None = None
        self._status_lock = threading.Lock()  # leaf lock: guards _status only
        self._status: dict[str, IntersectionStatus] = {}

    # ------------------------------------------------------------ wiring
    def register(self, controller: IntersectionController) -> None:
        if self._config.heartbeat_interval >= controller.heartbeat_timeout:
            raise InvalidConfigError(
                f"heartbeat_interval {self._config.heartbeat_interval}s must be shorter "
                f"than {controller.intersection_id}'s timeout {controller.heartbeat_timeout}s"
            )
        with self._lock:
            self._controllers[controller.intersection_id] = controller
        with self._status_lock:
            self._status[controller.intersection_id] = IntersectionStatus(None, None, None)

    # ------------------------------------------------------------- plans
    def set_plan(self, intersection_id: str, plan: TimingStrategy) -> bool:
        """Record the desired plan and try to deliver it now.
        Returns True if delivered, False if it will be retried."""
        with self._lock:
            self._controller(intersection_id)
            self._desired[intersection_id] = plan
            self._undelivered.add(intersection_id)
            return self._try_deliver(intersection_id)

    def set_plans(self, plans: Mapping[str, TimingStrategy]) -> None:
        for intersection_id, plan in plans.items():
            self.set_plan(intersection_id, plan)

    # ---------------------------------------------------------- incidents
    def report_incident(self, link_id: str, lanes_closed: int) -> None:
        """Close lanes on a link. Routing avoids fully closed links at once;
        the downstream controller learns its reduced capacity."""
        capacity = self._network.set_lanes_closed(link_id, lanes_closed)
        link = self._network.link(link_id)
        log.warning("incident on %s: %d lane(s) closed, capacity %s", link_id, lanes_closed, capacity)
        if capacity == 0:
            return  # nothing arrives on this approach any more
        with self._lock:
            controller = self._controller(link.to_id)
            try:
                self._send(link.to_id, lambda: controller.set_capacity(link.approach, capacity))
            except CommunicationLostError:
                log.warning("could not tell %s about the incident; link down", link.to_id)

    # ---------------------------------------------------------- emergency
    def dispatch_emergency(
        self, vehicle_id: str, origin: str, destination: str, speed_mps: float
    ) -> list[str]:
        """Plan a preemption run along the shortest open route.
        Returns the intersection ids the vehicle will pass, in order."""
        if speed_mps <= 0:
            raise EmergencyDispatchError("speed must be positive")
        route = self._network.shortest_route(origin, destination)
        now = self._clock.now()
        legs: list[_Leg] = []
        travelled = 0
        for link in route:
            travelled += link.length_m
            eta = now + math.ceil(travelled / speed_mps)
            legs.append(_Leg(link.to_id, link.approach, eta))
        with self._lock:
            if vehicle_id in self._runs:
                raise EmergencyDispatchError(f"{vehicle_id} is already dispatched")
            for leg in legs:
                self._controller(leg.intersection_id)
            self._runs[vehicle_id] = legs
        log.info("dispatched %s via %s", vehicle_id, [leg.intersection_id for leg in legs])
        return [leg.intersection_id for leg in legs]

    def emergency_legs(self, vehicle_id: str) -> list[tuple[str, LegState]]:
        with self._lock:
            legs = self._runs.get(vehicle_id)
            if legs is None:
                raise EmergencyDispatchError(f"unknown run {vehicle_id}")
            return [(leg.intersection_id, leg.state) for leg in legs]

    # --------------------------------------------------------- time passing
    def step(self) -> None:
        """Call once per simulated second, after the controllers have stepped."""
        now = self._clock.now()
        with self._lock:
            due = self._last_round is None or now - self._last_round >= self._config.heartbeat_interval
            if due:
                self._last_round = now
                self._heartbeat_round()
            self._advance_runs(now)

    # ------------------------------------------------------------ telemetry
    def on_event(self, event: TelemetryEvent) -> None:
        with self._status_lock:
            old = self._status.get(event.intersection_id, IntersectionStatus(None, None, None))
            phase = event.phase if isinstance(event, PhaseChanged) else old.phase
            mode = event.mode if isinstance(event, ModeChanged) else old.mode
            self._status[event.intersection_id] = IntersectionStatus(phase, mode, event.at)

    def status(self, intersection_id: str) -> IntersectionStatus:
        with self._status_lock:
            try:
                return self._status[intersection_id]
            except KeyError:
                raise UnknownIntersectionError(intersection_id) from None

    # ------------------------------------------------------------ internals
    def _controller(self, intersection_id: str) -> IntersectionController:
        try:
            return self._controllers[intersection_id]
        except KeyError:
            raise UnknownIntersectionError(intersection_id) from None

    def _send(self, intersection_id: str, command: Callable[[], object]) -> object:
        if not self._comms.is_up(intersection_id):
            raise CommunicationLostError(intersection_id)
        return command()

    def _try_deliver(self, intersection_id: str) -> bool:
        controller = self._controllers[intersection_id]
        plan = self._desired[intersection_id]
        try:
            self._send(intersection_id, lambda: controller.apply_plan(plan))
        except CommunicationLostError:
            return False
        self._undelivered.discard(intersection_id)
        return True

    def _heartbeat_round(self) -> None:
        for intersection_id in sorted(self._controllers):
            controller = self._controllers[intersection_id]
            try:
                self._send(intersection_id, controller.heartbeat)
            except CommunicationLostError:
                continue
            if intersection_id in self._undelivered:
                self._try_deliver(intersection_id)

    def _advance_runs(self, now: int) -> None:
        lead = self._config.preempt_lead_time
        after = self._config.preempt_release_after
        for vehicle_id, legs in self._runs.items():
            for leg in legs:
                controller = self._controllers[leg.intersection_id]
                if leg.state is LegState.PENDING and now >= leg.eta + after:
                    leg.state = LegState.MISSED
                    log.error("%s passed %s without preemption", vehicle_id, leg.intersection_id)
                elif leg.state is LegState.PENDING and now >= leg.eta - lead:
                    try:
                        self._send(leg.intersection_id,
                                   lambda: controller.preempt(leg.approach, vehicle_id))
                        leg.state = LegState.HELD
                    except TrafficError as exc:  # conflict, link down, fault flash
                        log.info("%s at %s waiting: %s", vehicle_id, leg.intersection_id, exc)
                elif leg.state is LegState.HELD and now >= leg.eta + after:
                    try:
                        self._send(leg.intersection_id, lambda: controller.release(vehicle_id))
                        leg.state = LegState.RELEASED
                    except CommunicationLostError:
                        pass  # retried next second; the controller's own hold limit backs us up

What to notice. register() refuses a heartbeat interval that isn't shorter than the controller's timeout. That misconfiguration would make every controller flap between central and fallback forever, and it is cheap to catch at wiring time. In _advance_runs(), a leg that can't be preempted yet stays PENDING and retries next second. A leg whose vehicle has already passed becomes MISSED and is logged at ERROR. That is the alert an operator needs to see.

Step 13 — The simulation loop

Goal: one second at a time, in a fixed order.

Type traffic_mgmt/simulation.py:

"""The deterministic world loop: one second at a time, in a fixed order.

Each second: advance the clock, step every controller (sorted by id),
then step the centre. Same inputs, same outputs, every run.
"""
import threading
from collections.abc import Mapping

from .clock import ManualClock
from .controller import IntersectionController
from .errors import UnknownIntersectionError
from .models import Axis
from .sensors import ApproachQueues
from .tmc import TrafficManagementCenter


class TrafficSimulation:
    def __init__(
        self,
        clock: ManualClock,
        tmc: TrafficManagementCenter,
        controllers: Mapping[str, IntersectionController],
        queues: Mapping[str, ApproachQueues],
    ) -> None:
        self._clock = clock
        self._tmc = tmc
        self._controllers = dict(sorted(controllers.items()))
        self._queues = dict(queues)
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # one world step at a time

    def add_arrivals(self, intersection_id: str, axis: Axis, vehicles: int) -> None:
        try:
            queues = self._queues[intersection_id]
        except KeyError:
            raise UnknownIntersectionError(intersection_id) from None
        queues.arrive(axis, vehicles)

    def run(self, seconds: int) -> None:
        if seconds < 0:
            raise ValueError("seconds must be >= 0")
        for _ in range(seconds):
            with self._lock:
                self._clock.advance(1)
                for controller in self._controllers.values():
                    controller.step()
                self._tmc.step()

What to notice. The lock is taken per second, not around the whole run(). Command threads can slip in between seconds, which is what a real controller cabinet sees: commands arrive between timer ticks.

def run(ctrl: IntersectionController, clock: ManualClock, seconds: int,
        heartbeat_every: int | None = None) -> None:
    for _ in range(seconds):
        clock.advance(1)
        ctrl.step()
        if heartbeat_every and clock.now() % heartbeat_every == 0:
            ctrl.heartbeat()

9. Common interview follow-ups

"Now add transit signal priority for buses." Priority is a softer cousin of preemption. A late bus may get a few extra seconds of green or an early green, but it never cuts a cross street short. The design change is a new request type, say PriorityRequest(axis, max_extension), and a controller method that adjusts _duration of the current or next green within a cap from config. Preemption still outranks it. The transition table does not change at all, because priority changes durations, not phases.

"Now an incident happens in the middle of an ambulance run." Today a run's legs are fixed at dispatch. The change is a reroute step in report_incident(). Find each active run whose remaining legs use the closed link. Recompute its route from the next pending intersection, release any held legs that are no longer on it, and splice in the new legs with fresh ETAs. The controller needs nothing new. That is the point of keeping routing in the centre and safety in the controller.

"Now scale to 10,000 intersections and three centre nodes." Partition intersections by region, one partition per node, with a message broker carrying heartbeats and telemetry. The desired-plan map moves into a database, and each plan gets a version number so apply_plan() becomes idempotent (ignore a version you already have). Controllers don't change. They already tolerate a silent centre, which is exactly what a node failover looks like from the roadside.

"Now broadcast signal timing to connected cars (SPaT)." Write a new TelemetryListener that turns PhaseChanged and CycleStarted events into "time to next change" messages. The controller, the centre and the bus are untouched. This is the Observer seam paying for itself.

"What if the centre is compromised and pushes a plan with one-second greens?" The controller clamps it to min_green and logs a warning; yellow and all-red were never the plan's to set. That bounds the damage to "slow". In a production design you would add signed plans and a local safety envelope per intersection (maximum cycle length, minimum pedestrian time), checked before apply_plan() stores anything.

"Two ambulances approach one junction on crossing streets." The first preemption wins and the second gets PreemptionConflictError, so the centre keeps its leg pending and retries every second. Once the first vehicle's hold is released, the second is served. A better policy orders conflicting requests by ETA and lets the earlier arrival go first even if it called second. That would be a new PreemptionPolicy strategy inside the controller.

"Why does a controller boot into ALL_RED and local fallback?" Because at power-up it knows nothing. All-red is the only phase that is safe without knowing who was moving a second ago. Local fallback is the only plan it can trust without the centre. It earns CENTRAL by hearing a heartbeat.

"Why not let the adaptive plan end a green early when the queue empties?" You could, and real "gap-out" logic does. It changes the controller, though, not the plan: the decision happens mid-phase, and plans in this design only speak at the cycle boundary. That is a deliberate trade. Plans stay simple and testable, and the cost is some wasted green on quiet approaches.
