Chapter 11: Expense Sharing (Splitwise)

Type every file in order. Run each checkpoint before moving on. All commands run from the project folder (the one holding splitwise/ and tests/). Python 3.13, stdlib only.

1. The problem

Four friends go on a trip. One pays for dinner, another pays for the hotel, a third pays for the taxi, and by Sunday nobody knows who owes whom. You are building the app that knows: Splitwise, or the core of it.

Users belong to groups. Someone pays an expense and says how to split it: equally, by exact amounts, by percentage, or by shares (two nights versus three). The app keeps a running balance between every pair of people, records settle-up payments when real money changes hands, and can propose a short list of payments that clears everything. Every change to money lands in an audit trail.

Underneath the friendly story sit three hard problems. Money must stay exact, so a $100 dinner split three ways produces $33.34, $33.33 and $33.33, and a stated rule decides who gets the extra cent. Concurrent writes must not lose a cent. And "minimise the number of payments" sounds like a small feature but is NP-hard, so you need an honest answer for it.

2. Requirements

Clarifying questions to ask first. Ask these before you draw a single box. The answers here are what a typical interviewer gives back.

  • Do non-group expenses exist, or does everything live in a group? Everything lives in a group. A two-person friendship is a group of two.
  • Single payer per expense, or several? Single payer for now. (Multiple payers is a goalpost move; see section 9.)
  • How is money represented, and who absorbs the odd cent? Your call, but you must say it out loud. Expect a follow-up if you reach for float.
  • Multiple currencies? Yes, but balances in different currencies are kept apart. Converting between them is a stretch goal.
  • Can expenses be edited or deleted? Deleted, yes, and the history must survive. Edit is a follow-up.
  • Can a settle-up be larger than what is owed? No. A payment may reduce debt but never create it.
  • Is "simplify debts" a view or a rewrite of history? A view. The raw pairwise history stays; the simplified plan is a suggestion.
  • Scale? Groups of tens of people, not thousands. Many groups in parallel.

The 2026 angle. Interviewers in 2026 still grade the classic rubric, but three things now get probed hard on this problem. First, money correctness: integer minor units or Decimal, a written rounding rule, and an invariant you can test. Second, honesty about complexity. Claim your greedy algorithm "minimises transactions" and you lose points. Say "greedy, at most n − 1 payments, optimal is NP-hard, here is a counterexample" and you gain them. Third, operational realism: audit trails, idempotent payments from flaky mobile clients, and what happens when two people tap "settle up" at the same moment. Many rounds now let you use an AI assistant, which raises the bar on those judgment calls. Anyone can generate a Splitwise class; the interview is about whether you can defend its rounding and its locks.

Functional

  • Register users; create groups; add members to a group.
  • Add an expense: payer, total, description, and a split strategy. Four strategies: equal, exact, percentage, shares.
  • Every split must sum to the total exactly. Exact splits that miss the total are rejected; percentages that miss 100 are rejected.
  • Track a balance per pair of users, per currency, inside each group. Show each user's net position.
  • Record settle-up payments. Reject paying yourself, non-positive amounts, and any payment that would create new debt.
  • Suggest a short settlement plan (debt simplification) per currency.
  • Delete an expense by reversing it. The record stays, flagged deleted.
  • Keep an append-only audit trail of every change.

Non-functional

  • Money is integer minor units; floats are rejected at the boundary. Rounding follows one stated rule: largest remainder, ties to the earlier-listed participant.
  • Thread-safe: concurrent expenses lose no cents; concurrent settle-ups cannot both pass the overpayment check.
  • Time and ids are injectable, so tests and the demo are deterministic.
  • Storage sits behind repository interfaces. Typed exception hierarchy; no bare except. Stdlib only.

5. Key design decisions

  1. Money is an int of minor units, and floats are refused at the door. 0.1 + 0.2 is not 0.3 in binary floating point. A ledger that drifts by a cent a week is a ledger nobody trusts. Money.of("12.34", USD) parses a string through Decimal, rejects anything with more decimal places than the currency allows, and stores 1234. JPY has zero decimal places, so "1.5" yen is an error, not a silent round.
  2. One rounding rule, written down: largest remainder. Every ratio split (equal, percent, shares) goes through allocate(). Each person first gets the floor of their exact share. The few cents left over go to the people with the largest fractional remainders, and ties go to whoever is listed first. That's why the $100 dinner comes out $33.34 / $33.33 / $33.33, with the extra cent on the first name. Exact Fraction arithmetic means no intermediate rounding sneaks in.
  3. The "sums to total" invariant lives in the base class. SplitStrategy.split() is a template method. Subclasses compute shares; the base checks currency, non-negativity and the sum. A fifth split kind gets the check for free, and a buggy one can't slip a lost cent past it.
  4. The ledger stores pairs, not people. One signed int per (currency, low id, high id). "Bea owes Awanish $30" and "Awanish owes Bea $12" land on the same row and cancel to $18 automatically. Net positions are derived from the pairs, so they always sum to zero; the ledger raises LedgerInvariantError if they ever don't.
  5. Settle-up is checked against net positions, not pairs. After simplification, people pay someone they never owed directly: Bea might pay Chen to clear a debt that ran Bea → Awanish → Chen. So the rule is "the payer must owe at least this much net, and the payee must be owed at least this much net". A payment can shrink debt but never invent it.
  6. Simplification is a greedy view, and the docs say so. Finding the minimum number of payments is NP-hard: it needs the largest number of disjoint zero-sum subgroups, a partition-style problem. GreedySimplifier pairs the largest debtor with the largest creditor, settles the smaller amount, and repeats. It is O(n log n), uses at most n − 1 payments, and is usually optimal. The tests pin a five-person case where it uses 4 payments and the optimum is 3.
  7. Delete is a reversal, never an erase. Deleting an expense posts its mirror image to the ledger and flags the record. The audit trail keeps both events. Accountants have done it this way for five hundred years, for good reason.
  8. One lock per group. Every ledger read and write for a group happens under that group's lock, including the audit append, so the trail's order matches the ledger's. "Check the net, then record the payment" is atomic. Different groups never wait on each other.

Patterns, and why each earns its place. Strategy twice: splits vary independently of booking money, and simplification algorithms vary independently of the ledger. Template method inside SplitStrategy, so the invariant can't be skipped. Repository for users, groups and expenses: the dicts become tables later. Value object for Money: immutable, compared by value, impossible to mix currencies by accident.

Step 2 — Money, and the one rounding rule

Goal: type Currency, Money, and allocate(). Every other file depends on these.

Type splitwise/money.py:

"""Money as integer minor units, plus the one rounding rule for splitting it.

Floats never touch money. An amount enters as a string or Decimal, is checked
for exact precision, and is stored as an int count of minor units (cents for
USD, yen for JPY). Splitting uses exact fractions and the largest-remainder
rule, so the parts always add back to the total.
"""

import math
from collections.abc import Sequence
from dataclasses import dataclass
from decimal import Decimal, InvalidOperation
from fractions import Fraction

from .errors import CurrencyMismatchError, InvalidAmountError, InvalidSplitError

CURRENCY_CODE_LENGTH = 3  # ISO 4217 codes are three letters


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class Currency:
    """An ISO 4217 currency and how many digits follow its decimal point."""

    code: str
    exponent: int

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if (
            len(self.code) != CURRENCY_CODE_LENGTH
            or not self.code.isalpha()
            or not self.code.isupper()
        ):
            raise InvalidAmountError(f"bad currency code {self.code!r}")
        if self.exponent < 0:
            raise InvalidAmountError(f"exponent must be >= 0, got {self.exponent}")


USD = Currency("USD", 2)
EUR = Currency("EUR", 2)
INR = Currency("INR", 2)
JPY = Currency("JPY", 0)


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class Money:
    """An exact amount: an int count of minor units in one currency."""

    minor: int
    currency: Currency

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if isinstance(self.minor, bool) or not isinstance(self.minor, int):
            raise InvalidAmountError(f"minor units must be an int, got {self.minor!r}")

    @classmethod
    def of(cls, amount: str | Decimal | int, currency: Currency) -> "Money":
        """Parse a human amount like "12.34". Rejects floats and extra decimal places."""
        if isinstance(amount, float):
            raise InvalidAmountError("pass money as a str or Decimal, never a float")
        try:
            value = Decimal(amount)
        except (InvalidOperation, TypeError, ValueError) as exc:
            raise InvalidAmountError(f"not an amount: {amount!r}") from exc
        if not value.is_finite():
            raise InvalidAmountError(f"not a finite amount: {amount!r}")
        scaled = value.scaleb(currency.exponent)
        if scaled != scaled.to_integral_value():
            raise InvalidAmountError(
                f"{amount} has more than {currency.exponent} decimal places for {currency.code}"
            )
        return cls(int(scaled), currency)

    @classmethod
    def zero(cls, currency: Currency) -> "Money":
        return cls(0, currency)

    def _same_currency(self, other: "Money") -> None:
        if self.currency != other.currency:
            raise CurrencyMismatchError(
                f"cannot combine {self.currency.code} with {other.currency.code}"
            )

    def __add__(self, other: "Money") -> "Money":
        self._same_currency(other)
        return Money(self.minor + other.minor, self.currency)

    def __sub__(self, other: "Money") -> "Money":
        self._same_currency(other)
        return Money(self.minor - other.minor, self.currency)

    def __neg__(self) -> "Money":
        return Money(-self.minor, self.currency)

    def is_positive(self) -> bool:
        return self.minor > 0

    def __str__(self) -> str:
        exp = self.currency.exponent
        return f"{self.currency.code} {Decimal(self.minor).scaleb(-exp):.{exp}f}"


def allocate(total_minor: int, weights: Sequence[Fraction]) -> list[int]:
    """Split total_minor in proportion to weights. The parts always sum to total_minor.

    Rounding rule (largest remainder): each part first gets the floor of its
    exact share. The few minor units left over go one each to the parts with
    the largest fractional remainders. Ties go to the earlier position, so the
    caller's participant order decides who absorbs the odd cent.
    """
    if not weights:
        raise InvalidSplitError("nothing to allocate between")
    if any(w < 0 for w in weights):
        raise InvalidSplitError("weights must not be negative")
    weight_sum = sum(weights, Fraction(0))
    if weight_sum == 0:
        raise InvalidSplitError("at least one weight must be positive")
    exact = [Fraction(total_minor) * w / weight_sum for w in weights]
    parts = [math.floor(e) for e in exact]
    leftover = total_minor - sum(parts)
    by_remainder = sorted(range(len(parts)), key=lambda i: (-(exact[i] - parts[i]), i))
    for i in by_remainder[:leftover]:
        parts[i] += 1
    return parts

What to notice. Three moves. Money.of rejects float by type, before any conversion can happen, so Money.of(0.1, USD) fails loudly. scaled != scaled.to_integral_value() is the precision check: "1.005" scaled by 100 is 100.5, not an integer, so it's refused. And allocate sorts by (-remainder, position). That tuple is the rounding rule: biggest remainder first, earliest position on a tie.

"""Injectable time and ids, so tests and demos are deterministic."""

import itertools
import threading
import uuid
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from datetime import UTC, datetime


class Clock(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def now(self) -> datetime:
        """Current time, timezone-aware UTC."""


class SystemClock(Clock):
    def now(self) -> datetime:
        return datetime.now(UTC)


class FixedClock(Clock):
    """Test clock: returns a set time until advanced. Not thread-safe; tests only."""

    def __init__(self, at: datetime) -> None:
        self._at = at

    def now(self) -> datetime:
        return self._at

    def set(self, at: datetime) -> None:
        self._at = at


class IdGenerator(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def next_id(self, prefix: str) -> str:
        """A fresh id such as 'exp-3'. Never repeats within one generator."""


class UuidIds(IdGenerator):
    def next_id(self, prefix: str) -> str:
        return f"{prefix}-{uuid.uuid4().hex[:12]}"


class SequentialIds(IdGenerator):
    """Readable ids, one counter per prefix. The lock guards the counter map."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()
        self._counters: dict[str, itertools.count[int]] = {}

    def next_id(self, prefix: str) -> str:
        with self._lock:
            counter = self._counters.setdefault(prefix, itertools.count(1))
            return f"{prefix}-{next(counter)}"

What to notice. Ids are injected for the same reason as time. Random ids make demo output change every run; SequentialIds gives exp-1, exp-2 so the expected output in this chapter is exactly what you'll see. Its counter map is shared state, so it gets a lock.

Step 6 — Split strategies

Goal: type the SplitStrategy base with its invariant check, then the four kinds.

Type splitwise/splits.py:

"""Split strategies: how one expense total becomes what each participant owes.

Template method: SplitStrategy.split() asks the subclass for the parts, then
checks the invariant every split must keep. The parts sum to the total,
exactly, in the total's currency. A new split kind is a new subclass; the
invariant check comes free.
"""

from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from collections.abc import Mapping, Sequence
from decimal import Decimal, InvalidOperation
from fractions import Fraction
from typing import ClassVar

from .errors import InvalidAmountError, InvalidSplitError
from .money import Money, allocate

PERCENT_WHOLE = Fraction(100)  # percentages must add up to exactly this


class SplitStrategy(ABC):
    """Base class. Subclasses compute parts; this class guards the invariant."""

    kind: ClassVar[str]

    @property
    @abstractmethod
    def participants(self) -> tuple[str, ...]:
        """Who shares the expense, in the order used to break rounding ties."""

    @abstractmethod
    def _parts(self, total: Money) -> dict[str, Money]:
        """Each participant's share. May assume total is positive."""

    def split(self, total: Money) -> dict[str, Money]:
        if not total.is_positive():
            raise InvalidAmountError(f"an expense total must be positive, got {total}")
        parts = self._parts(total)
        for user_id, part in parts.items():
            if part.currency != total.currency:
                raise InvalidSplitError(
                    f"{user_id}'s share is in {part.currency.code}, "
                    f"the total is in {total.currency.code}"
                )
            if part.minor < 0:
                raise InvalidSplitError(f"{user_id}'s share is negative: {part}")
        owed = sum(p.minor for p in parts.values())
        if owed != total.minor:
            raise InvalidSplitError(
                f"shares sum to {Money(owed, total.currency)} but the total is {total}"
            )
        return parts


def _unique(ids: Sequence[str]) -> tuple[str, ...]:
    if not ids:
        raise InvalidSplitError("a split needs at least one participant")
    if len(set(ids)) != len(ids):
        raise InvalidSplitError(f"duplicate participants in {list(ids)}")
    return tuple(ids)


class EqualSplit(SplitStrategy):
    """Everyone owes the same; the odd cents go to the earliest-listed participants."""

    kind = "equal"

    def __init__(self, participants: Sequence[str]) -> None:
        self._participants = _unique(participants)

    @property
    def participants(self) -> tuple[str, ...]:
        return self._participants

    def _parts(self, total: Money) -> dict[str, Money]:
        minors = allocate(total.minor, [Fraction(1)] * len(self._participants))
        return {
            u: Money(m, total.currency) for u, m in zip(self._participants, minors, strict=True)
        }


class ExactSplit(SplitStrategy):
    """The caller states each amount. The base class rejects them if they miss the total."""

    kind = "exact"

    def __init__(self, amounts: Mapping[str, Money]) -> None:
        self._participants = _unique(list(amounts))
        self._amounts = dict(amounts)

    @property
    def participants(self) -> tuple[str, ...]:
        return self._participants

    def _parts(self, total: Money) -> dict[str, Money]:
        return dict(self._amounts)


class PercentSplit(SplitStrategy):
    """Each participant owes a percentage. Percentages must add up to exactly 100."""

    kind = "percent"

    def __init__(self, percents: Mapping[str, str | Decimal]) -> None:
        self._participants = _unique(list(percents))
        self._weights: list[Fraction] = []
        for user_id, raw in percents.items():
            if isinstance(raw, float):
                raise InvalidSplitError("pass percentages as str or Decimal, never float")
            try:
                weight = Fraction(Decimal(raw))
            except (InvalidOperation, TypeError, ValueError) as exc:
                raise InvalidSplitError(f"{user_id}: not a percentage: {raw!r}") from exc
            if weight < 0:
                raise InvalidSplitError(f"{user_id}: percentage must not be negative")
            self._weights.append(weight)
        if sum(self._weights, Fraction(0)) != PERCENT_WHOLE:
            raise InvalidSplitError(f"percentages sum to {float(sum(self._weights))}, not 100")

    @property
    def participants(self) -> tuple[str, ...]:
        return self._participants

    def _parts(self, total: Money) -> dict[str, Money]:
        minors = allocate(total.minor, self._weights)
        return {
            u: Money(m, total.currency) for u, m in zip(self._participants, minors, strict=True)
        }


class SharesSplit(SplitStrategy):
    """Each participant owes in proportion to a whole number of shares (2 nights vs 3)."""

    kind = "shares"

    def __init__(self, shares: Mapping[str, int]) -> None:
        self._participants = _unique(list(shares))
        for user_id, count in shares.items():
            if isinstance(count, bool) or not isinstance(count, int) or count < 1:
                raise InvalidSplitError(f"{user_id}: shares must be a positive int, got {count!r}")
        self._weights = [Fraction(c) for c in shares.values()]

    @property
    def participants(self) -> tuple[str, ...]:
        return self._participants

    def _parts(self, total: Money) -> dict[str, Money]:
        minors = allocate(total.minor, self._weights)
        return {
            u: Money(m, total.currency) for u, m in zip(self._participants, minors, strict=True)
        }

Percentages arrive as strings and go Decimal → Fraction, so "33.33" means exactly 33.33.

Step 7 — The balance ledger

Goal: type BalanceLedger, one signed int per (currency, pair), with net positions derived from it.

Type splitwise/ledger.py:

"""The balance ledger: who owes whom, per pair of users, per currency.

One signed int per (currency, pair). The pair key is sorted, so A-owes-B and
B-owes-A are the same row with opposite signs; they cancel automatically.

Not thread-safe on its own. ExpenseService holds the group's lock around
every call, because its check-then-act steps span several ledger calls.
"""

from collections import defaultdict

from .errors import LedgerInvariantError
from .models import Debt
from .money import Currency, Money


class BalanceLedger:
    """Pairwise balances for one group."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        # (currency code, low id, high id) -> minor units. Positive: low owes high.
        self._pairs: dict[tuple[str, str, str], int] = defaultdict(int)
        self._currencies: dict[str, Currency] = {}

    def record_debt(self, debtor: str, creditor: str, amount: Money) -> None:
        """debtor now owes creditor `amount` more. Owing yourself is a no-op."""
        if debtor == creditor or amount.minor == 0:
            return
        self._currencies[amount.currency.code] = amount.currency
        low, high = sorted((debtor, creditor))
        sign = 1 if debtor == low else -1
        key = (amount.currency.code, low, high)
        self._pairs[key] += sign * amount.minor
        if self._pairs[key] == 0:
            del self._pairs[key]

    def owed(self, debtor: str, creditor: str, currency: Currency) -> Money:
        """How much debtor owes creditor directly (zero if it runs the other way)."""
        low, high = sorted((debtor, creditor))
        raw = self._pairs.get((currency.code, low, high), 0)
        signed = raw if debtor == low else -raw
        return Money(max(signed, 0), currency)

    def debts(self, currency: Currency) -> list[Debt]:
        """Every nonzero pairwise balance in this currency, sorted for stable output."""
        out = []
        for (code, low, high), raw in sorted(self._pairs.items()):
            if code != currency.code:
                continue
            if raw > 0:
                out.append(Debt(low, high, Money(raw, currency)))
            else:
                out.append(Debt(high, low, Money(-raw, currency)))
        return out

    def net(self, currency: Currency) -> dict[str, Money]:
        """Per-user net position. Positive: the group owes them. Always sums to zero."""
        totals: dict[str, int] = defaultdict(int)
        for (code, low, high), raw in self._pairs.items():
            if code == currency.code:
                totals[low] -= raw
                totals[high] += raw
        if sum(totals.values()) != 0:
            raise LedgerInvariantError(f"{currency.code} nets sum to {sum(totals.values())}")
        return {u: Money(v, currency) for u, v in sorted(totals.items()) if v != 0}

    def currencies(self) -> list[Currency]:
        """Currencies with at least one open balance."""
        open_codes = {code for code, _, _ in self._pairs}
        return [c for code, c in sorted(self._currencies.items()) if code in open_codes]

What to notice. The sorted pair key is the whole trick. Without it you'd store "A owes B" and "B owes A" as two rows and need a reconciliation step. With it, opposite debts net out on write. Zero rows are deleted, so debts() never shows a "$0.00" line. The ledger has no lock of its own: the service's group lock already wraps every multi-step operation, and a second lock here would only invite lock-ordering bugs.

Step 8 — Debt simplification

Goal: type the DebtSimplifier strategy and the greedy algorithm.

Type splitwise/simplify.py:

"""Debt simplification: turn net balances into a short list of payments.

The minimum number of payments is NP-hard to find in general. It needs the
largest number of disjoint zero-sum subgroups, which is a partition-style
problem. GreedySimplifier is the practical answer: at most n - 1 payments,
O(n log n), and nobody pays more than they owe or receives more than they are
owed. It is not always minimal; the tests pin a case where it is not.
"""

import heapq
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from collections.abc import Mapping

from .errors import CurrencyMismatchError, LedgerInvariantError
from .models import Debt
from .money import Money


class DebtSimplifier(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def simplify(self, net: Mapping[str, Money]) -> list[Debt]:
        """Payments that bring every net balance to zero. Positive net: is owed."""


class GreedySimplifier(DebtSimplifier):
    """Largest debtor pays largest creditor the smaller of the two amounts. Repeat."""

    def simplify(self, net: Mapping[str, Money]) -> list[Debt]:
        if not net:
            return []
        currency = next(iter(net.values())).currency
        if any(m.currency != currency for m in net.values()):
            raise CurrencyMismatchError("simplify one currency at a time")
        if sum(m.minor for m in net.values()) != 0:
            raise LedgerInvariantError("net balances must sum to zero")
        # heapq is a min-heap, so store negated amounts to pop the largest first.
        # The user id breaks ties, which keeps the output deterministic.
        creditors = [(-m.minor, u) for u, m in net.items() if m.minor > 0]
        debtors = [(m.minor, u) for u, m in net.items() if m.minor < 0]
        heapq.heapify(creditors)
        heapq.heapify(debtors)
        payments: list[Debt] = []
        while creditors and debtors:
            credit, creditor = heapq.heappop(creditors)
            debit, debtor = heapq.heappop(debtors)
            credit, debit = -credit, -debit
            paid = min(credit, debit)
            payments.append(Debt(debtor, creditor, Money(paid, currency)))
            if credit > paid:
                heapq.heappush(creditors, (-(credit - paid), creditor))
            if debit > paid:
                heapq.heappush(debtors, (-(debit - paid), debtor))
        return payments

What to notice. Each round settles at least one person completely, which is why the plan never exceeds n − 1 payments. But "at most n − 1" isn't "minimal". In the checkpoint below, d owes exactly what a is owed ($60), so the best plan is d → a, then e and f pay b: three payments. Greedy grabs the biggest numbers first (d pays b), breaks that perfect match, and needs four. Finding the true minimum means searching for zero-sum subgroups, which is NP-hard. Say this in the interview before you're asked.

Step 9 — The audit trail

Goal: an append-only log with no update and no delete.

Type splitwise/audit.py:

"""Append-only audit trail. Every change to money gets one entry, forever."""

import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from dataclasses import dataclass
from datetime import datetime
from enum import Enum


class AuditAction(Enum):
    USER_REGISTERED = "user_registered"
    GROUP_CREATED = "group_created"
    MEMBER_ADDED = "member_added"
    EXPENSE_ADDED = "expense_added"
    EXPENSE_DELETED = "expense_deleted"
    SETTLEMENT_RECORDED = "settlement_recorded"


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class AuditEntry:
    seq: int
    at: datetime
    actor: str
    action: AuditAction
    subject_id: str
    detail: str

    def __str__(self) -> str:
        what = f"{self.action.value} {self.subject_id}"
        return f"#{self.seq} {self.at:%H:%M} {self.actor} {what}: {self.detail}"


class AuditLog(ABC):
    """Append and read. There is deliberately no update and no delete."""

    @abstractmethod
    def append(
        self, at: datetime, actor: str, action: AuditAction, subject_id: str, detail: str
    ) -> AuditEntry: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def entries(self, subject_id: str | None = None) -> tuple[AuditEntry, ...]: ...


class InMemoryAuditLog(AuditLog):
    """The lock guards _entries and keeps seq numbers gap-free and ordered."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._lock = threading.Lock()
        self._entries: list[AuditEntry] = []

    def append(
        self, at: datetime, actor: str, action: AuditAction, subject_id: str, detail: str
    ) -> AuditEntry:
        with self._lock:
            entry = AuditEntry(len(self._entries) + 1, at, actor, action, subject_id, detail)
            self._entries.append(entry)
            return entry

    def entries(self, subject_id: str | None = None) -> tuple[AuditEntry, ...]:
        with self._lock:
            return tuple(
                e for e in self._entries if subject_id is None or e.subject_id == subject_id
            )

What to notice. The interface is the guarantee. AuditLog has append and entries, and nothing else, so no caller can rewrite history even by accident. The sequence number is assigned under the lock, so it's gap-free and ordered even with many writers.

import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod

from .errors import DuplicateIdError, UnknownExpenseError, UnknownGroupError, UnknownUserError

class UserRepository(ABC):

    def get(self, user_id: str) -> User: ...

    def get(self, group_id: str) -> Group: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def update(self, group: Group) -> None: ...


class ExpenseRepository(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def add(self, expense: Expense) -> None: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def get(self, expense_id: str) -> Expense: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def update(self, expense: Expense) -> None: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def for_group(self, group_id: str) -> list[Expense]: ...

class InMemoryUsers(UserRepository):
    def __init__(self) -> None:

        self._users: dict[str, User] = {}

    def add(self, user: User) -> None:

Step 11 — The service

Goal: type ExpenseService. It wires everything together and owns the per-group locks.

Type splitwise/service.py:

"""ExpenseService: the one front door for users, groups, expenses, and settle-ups.

Concurrency: one lock per group. Every read or write of a group's ledger,
membership, or expenses happens under that group's lock, so check-then-act
steps (is this payment an overpayment? then record it) are atomic. The audit
entry is written under the same lock, so the trail's order matches the order
the ledger changed. Different groups never contend. _locks_guard only
protects the lock and ledger maps.
"""

import logging
import threading
from collections.abc import Iterable

from .audit import AuditAction, AuditEntry, AuditLog, InMemoryAuditLog
from .clock import Clock, IdGenerator, SystemClock, UuidIds
from .config import SplitwiseConfig
from .errors import (
    ExpenseAlreadyDeletedError,
    InvalidAmountError,
    InvalidInputError,
    InvalidSettlementError,
    InvalidSplitError,
    NotAGroupMemberError,
    OverpaymentError,
    UnknownGroupError,
    UnsupportedCurrencyError,
)
from .ledger import BalanceLedger
from .models import Debt, Expense, Group, Settlement, User
from .money import Currency, Money
from .repositories import (
    ExpenseRepository,
    GroupRepository,
    InMemoryExpenses,
    InMemoryGroups,
    InMemoryUsers,
    UserRepository,
)
from .simplify import DebtSimplifier, GreedySimplifier
from .splits import SplitStrategy

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)


class ExpenseService:
    def __init__(
        self,
        config: SplitwiseConfig | None = None,
        users: UserRepository | None = None,
        groups: GroupRepository | None = None,
        expenses: ExpenseRepository | None = None,
        audit: AuditLog | None = None,
        simplifier: DebtSimplifier | None = None,
        clock: Clock | None = None,
        ids: IdGenerator | None = None,
    ) -> None:
        self._config = config or SplitwiseConfig()
        self._users = users or InMemoryUsers()
        self._groups = groups or InMemoryGroups()
        self._expenses = expenses or InMemoryExpenses()
        self._audit = audit or InMemoryAuditLog()
        self._simplifier = simplifier or GreedySimplifier()
        self._clock = clock or SystemClock()
        self._ids = ids or UuidIds()
        self._locks_guard = threading.Lock()
        self._group_locks: dict[str, threading.Lock] = {}
        self._ledgers: dict[str, BalanceLedger] = {}

    # ----- users and groups -------------------------------------------------

    def register_user(self, user_id: str, name: str) -> User:
        user = User(user_id, name)
        self._users.add(user)
        self._audit.append(self._clock.now(), user_id, AuditAction.USER_REGISTERED, user_id, name)
        log.info("registered user %s", user_id)
        return user

    def create_group(self, name: str, created_by: str, members: Iterable[str] = ()) -> Group:
        member_ids = frozenset(members) | {created_by}
        for user_id in member_ids:
            self._users.get(user_id)  # raises UnknownUserError
        group = Group(self._ids.next_id("grp"), name, member_ids)
        with self._locks_guard:
            self._group_locks[group.group_id] = threading.Lock()
            self._ledgers[group.group_id] = BalanceLedger()
        self._groups.add(group)
        self._audit.append(
            self._clock.now(),
            created_by,
            AuditAction.GROUP_CREATED,
            group.group_id,
            f"{name!r} with {', '.join(sorted(member_ids))}",
        )
        log.info("created group %s", group.group_id)
        return group

    def add_member(self, group_id: str, user_id: str, actor: str) -> Group:
        self._users.get(user_id)
        lock, _ = self._group_state(group_id)
        with lock:
            group = self._groups.get(group_id)
            self._require_members(group, [actor])
            updated = group.with_member(user_id)
            self._groups.update(updated)
            self._audit.append(
                self._clock.now(), actor, AuditAction.MEMBER_ADDED, group_id, user_id
            )
        return updated

    # ----- expenses ---------------------------------------------------------

    def add_expense(
        self, group_id: str, payer_id: str, total: Money, split: SplitStrategy, description: str
    ) -> Expense:
        self._check_description(description)
        self._check_currency(total.currency)
        if len(split.participants) > self._config.max_participants:
            raise InvalidSplitError(
                f"at most {self._config.max_participants} participants per expense"
            )
        shares = split.split(total)  # validates: parts sum to total
        lock, ledger = self._group_state(group_id)
        with lock:
            group = self._groups.get(group_id)
            self._require_members(group, [payer_id, *split.participants])
            expense = Expense(
                self._ids.next_id("exp"),
                group_id,
                description.strip(),
                payer_id,
                total,
                tuple(shares.items()),
                split.kind,
                self._clock.now(),
            )
            self._expenses.add(expense)
            for user_id, share in shares.items():
                ledger.record_debt(user_id, payer_id, share)
            self._audit.append(
                expense.created_at,
                payer_id,
                AuditAction.EXPENSE_ADDED,
                expense.expense_id,
                f"{description.strip()!r} {total} {split.kind} split in {group_id}",
            )
        log.info("expense %s: %s paid %s in %s", expense.expense_id, payer_id, total, group_id)
        return expense

    def delete_expense(self, expense_id: str, actor: str) -> Expense:
        """Reverse an expense's effect on balances. The record stays, flagged deleted."""
        expense = self._expenses.get(expense_id)
        lock, ledger = self._group_state(expense.group_id)
        with lock:
            expense = self._expenses.get(expense_id)  # re-read under the lock
            if expense.deleted:
                raise ExpenseAlreadyDeletedError(expense_id)
            self._require_members(self._groups.get(expense.group_id), [actor])
            for user_id, share in expense.shares:
                ledger.record_debt(expense.payer_id, user_id, share)  # the mirror image
            deleted = Expense(
                expense.expense_id,
                expense.group_id,
                expense.description,
                expense.payer_id,
                expense.total,
                expense.shares,
                expense.split_kind,
                expense.created_at,
                deleted=True,
            )
            self._expenses.update(deleted)
            self._audit.append(
                self._clock.now(),
                actor,
                AuditAction.EXPENSE_DELETED,
                expense_id,
                f"reversed {expense.total} paid by {expense.payer_id}",
            )
        log.info("expense %s deleted by %s", expense_id, actor)
        return deleted

    # ----- settling up ------------------------------------------------------

    def settle_up(self, group_id: str, payer_id: str, payee_id: str, amount: Money) -> Settlement:
        """Record a real payment. It may reduce debt but never create or flip it."""
        if payer_id == payee_id:
            raise InvalidSettlementError("cannot pay yourself")
        if not amount.is_positive():
            raise InvalidAmountError(f"a payment must be positive, got {amount}")
        self._check_currency(amount.currency)
        lock, ledger = self._group_state(group_id)
        with lock:
            self._require_members(self._groups.get(group_id), [payer_id, payee_id])
            net = ledger.net(amount.currency)
            zero = Money.zero(amount.currency)
            payer_owes = -net.get(payer_id, zero).minor
            payee_is_owed = net.get(payee_id, zero).minor
            if amount.minor > payer_owes or amount.minor > payee_is_owed:
                raise OverpaymentError(
                    f"{payer_id} owes {Money(max(payer_owes, 0), amount.currency)} net and "
                    f"{payee_id} is owed {Money(max(payee_is_owed, 0), amount.currency)} net; "
                    f"a payment of {amount} would create new debt"
                )
            settlement = Settlement(
                self._ids.next_id("pay"), group_id, payer_id, payee_id, amount, self._clock.now()
            )
            self._expenses.add_settlement(settlement)
            ledger.record_debt(payee_id, payer_id, amount)  # payee now owes payer back
            self._audit.append(
                settlement.created_at,
                payer_id,
                AuditAction.SETTLEMENT_RECORDED,
                settlement.settlement_id,
                f"{payer_id} paid {payee_id} {amount} in {group_id}",
            )
        log.info(
            "settlement %s: %s paid %s %s", settlement.settlement_id, payer_id, payee_id, amount
        )
        return settlement

    # ----- reading balances -------------------------------------------------

    def net_balances(self, group_id: str, currency: Currency) -> dict[str, Money]:
        lock, ledger = self._group_state(group_id)
        with lock:
            return ledger.net(currency)

    def debts(self, group_id: str, currency: Currency) -> list[Debt]:
        """Pairwise view: who owes whom directly, from the raw expense history."""
        lock, ledger = self._group_state(group_id)
        with lock:
            return ledger.debts(currency)

    def suggest_settlements(self, group_id: str, currency: Currency) -> list[Debt]:
        """Simplified view: a short list of payments that zeroes every net balance."""
        return self._simplifier.simplify(self.net_balances(group_id, currency))

    def open_currencies(self, group_id: str) -> list[Currency]:
        lock, ledger = self._group_state(group_id)
        with lock:
            return ledger.currencies()

    def audit_trail(self, subject_id: str | None = None) -> tuple[AuditEntry, ...]:
        return self._audit.entries(subject_id)

    # ----- helpers ----------------------------------------------------------

    def _group_state(self, group_id: str) -> tuple[threading.Lock, BalanceLedger]:
        with self._locks_guard:
            if group_id not in self._group_locks:
                raise UnknownGroupError(group_id)
            return self._group_locks[group_id], self._ledgers[group_id]

    @staticmethod
    def _require_members(group: Group, user_ids: Iterable[str]) -> None:
        outsiders = sorted(set(user_ids) - group.members)
        if outsiders:
            raise NotAGroupMemberError(f"{', '.join(outsiders)} not in group {group.group_id}")

    def _check_currency(self, currency: Currency) -> None:
        if currency not in self._config.currencies:
            raise UnsupportedCurrencyError(currency.code)

    def _check_description(self, description: str) -> None:
        if not description or not description.strip():
            raise InvalidInputError("description must not be blank")
        if len(description.strip()) > self._config.max_description_length:
            raise InvalidInputError(
                f"description longer than {self._config.max_description_length} chars"
            )

What to notice. Look at what happens outside the lock in add_expense: description, currency and the split itself are validated first. Pure checks don't need the lock, and keeping them out keeps the critical section short. Inside the lock sit only the steps that read or change shared state. In settle_up, the overpayment check and the ledger write sit in the same with lock: block. Split them, and two taps on "settle up" could both pass the check. In delete_expense, the expense is re-read under the lock, so two concurrent deletes can't both see deleted=False.

9. Common interview follow-ups

"Why not just use float and round at the end?" Because "the end" never comes in a ledger. Balances are updated thousands of times, and each float operation can add error. Integer minor units make every addition exact, and the only rounding happens once, inside allocate, under a rule you can state. Decimal would also work; ints are simpler and faster, and the parse step already uses Decimal.

"Who gets the extra cent, and is that fair?" The earliest-listed participant on a tie, after anyone with a larger fractional remainder. Over many expenses it's fair if the UI rotates the order, or lists the payer first so the payer absorbs it. The key point: it's deterministic and written down, so two servers computing the same split always agree.

Goalpost: "Now support multiple payers on one expense." The payer side becomes a split too. Change Expense.payer_id to a paid tuple of (user, Money) validated with the same "sums to total" rule (an ExactSplit over payers). Posting to the ledger changes: each participant's owed share is spread across the payers in proportion to what each paid, using allocate again so cents still add up. Net positions stay "paid minus owed", and nothing in BalanceLedger, the simplifier or settle-up changes.

Goalpost: "Now let users edit an expense." Edit is delete plus add: reverse the old version, post the new one, and link them with a supersedes id and a version number. The audit trail then shows both. To stop two people editing at once, add optimistic concurrency: the edit request carries the version it read, and the service rejects it under the group lock if the version moved.

Goalpost: "Now convert currencies when settling." Keep balances per currency; never convert them silently, because rates move and nobody agreed to a rate. Add an FxRateProvider strategy (injected, like the clock). A cross-currency settle-up records the rate it used and posts two ledger entries: the payment in the payee's currency, and the converted reduction in the debt's currency. The rate snapshot goes into the Settlement and the audit entry, so the conversion can be explained later.

Goalpost: "The mobile app retries on timeout. Payments are being recorded twice." Add an idempotency key. The client generates a key per tap; settle_up takes it; the repository stores processed keys and returns the original Settlement on a repeat instead of recording a new one. The check and the insert go under the same group lock, or a unique constraint in the database.

Goalpost: "Give me the true minimum number of payments." Say it's NP-hard first. Then offer the exact algorithm for small groups: the minimum is n minus the largest number of disjoint zero-sum subsets, which a bitmask DP over subsets finds in O(3^n), fine up to about 15 people. Keep greedy as the fallback above that. That's a new DebtSimplifier subclass; the service doesn't change.

"Two app servers behind a load balancer?" In-memory locks don't cross processes. Store the ledger as an append-only table of entries (double-entry style) and serialise per group with SELECT ... FOR UPDATE on the group row, or use serializable transactions. Balances become a materialised view or a cached sum. The per-group lock in this code maps one-to-one onto that row lock.

"Can someone leave a group?" Only with a zero net in every currency. Otherwise their debts would orphan. Add remove_member that checks net_balances for each open currency under the group lock and raises a typed error if anything is open.
