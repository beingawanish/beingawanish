Type every file in order. Run each checkpoint before moving on. All commands run from the project folder (the one holding ride_sharing/ and tests/). Python 3.13, stdlib only.

1. The problem

A rider opens the app, drops a pin, and asks for a car. Somewhere nearby a few drivers are idling with the app open. The system has to pick one of them, offer the trip, and wait. The driver can accept, decline, or ignore the offer until it times out. If they don't accept, the next driver gets it. Once someone accepts, the trip moves through a fixed life: matched, driver arriving, in progress, completed. It can be cancelled along the way, sometimes for a fee. When it ends, a fare is charged, possibly with surge pricing because demand outran supply, and both sides rate each other.

That's the whole story. The hard part hides in one sentence: a driver must never be handed two riders at once. Requests arrive concurrently, drivers flip online and offline at any moment, and offers expire on a clock. Most of this chapter is about making that one guarantee hold while everything else stays pluggable.

Scope for the interview: one city, one process, a flat grid measured in kilometres. No maps, no routing engine, no payments gateway. Those are seams you name, not code you write.

2. Requirements

Questions to ask before you design. Ride-hailing is one of the most common LLD prompts in 2026 loops, and interviewers now grade the asking as hard as the code. These are the questions they expect, with the answers this chapter assumes:

  • How is "best driver" decided: distance, ETA, rating? Pluggable. Ship nearest-first and a rating-weighted variant.
  • Do we offer to one driver at a time, or broadcast to several? One at a time, with a timeout. Broadcast is a follow-up.
  • How long does a driver have to answer? 15 seconds, from config.
  • What happens when nobody accepts? Re-offer to the next driver, up to 3 attempts. Then cancel the trip as "no driver available."
  • How does surge work? A multiplier from open requests versus free drivers near the pickup, capped at 2.5x.
  • Is the fare upfront or metered? Quote upfront from distance and an estimated duration. Charge at the end from the real duration, with the surge locked at request time.
  • When does cancelling cost money? The rider pays a flat fee only if they cancel more than two minutes after a driver accepts.
  • Who rates whom? Both directions, 1 to 5 stars, once per trip, only after completion.
  • What scale? One process for the interview. Be ready to talk about many.

Functional

  • Register riders and drivers. Drivers go online and offline and report their location.
  • A rider requests a ride from a pickup to a dropoff. The request is rejected for an unknown rider or a pickup equal to the dropoff.
  • Find free drivers within a search radius through a spatial index, not a scan of every driver.
  • Rank candidates with a pluggable matching strategy: nearest available, or rating-weighted.
  • Offer the trip to one driver. The driver accepts, declines, or lets it time out. Decline and timeout re-offer to the next untried driver.
  • Trip state machine: REQUESTED → MATCHED → DRIVER_ARRIVING → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED, with CANCELLED reachable from the first three. Any other move raises IllegalTransitionError.
  • Fare = max(base + per-km + per-minute, minimum) × surge. Surge comes from supply and demand at request time.
  • Cancellation fee per a pluggable policy.
  • Ratings in both directions, with running averages.
  • Observers receive every trip event (requested, offered, declined, expired, matched, arriving, started, completed, cancelled).

Non-functional

  • One driver is never offered or assigned two trips at once, under any thread interleaving.
  • Time is injectable. Offer deadlines, the free-cancel window and trip durations all read the clock.
  • Location pings are the hot path. They must not wait on the dispatch lock.
  • A failing observer never breaks a ride.
  • Typed exception hierarchy, stdlib logging, every tunable in a config dataclass, stdlib only.

5. Key design decisions

  1. The driver claim is a compare-and-set. DriverRepository.claim(driver_id, expected, new) flips a driver's status only if it is still expected, all under the repository's lock. Dispatch ranks a snapshot of drivers, then claims the winner. If the claim fails (the driver just went offline, or someone else got them), dispatch simply tries the next one. This is the line that makes double-assignment impossible, and it's the same shape as UPDATE drivers SET status='OFFERED' WHERE id=? AND status='AVAILABLE' in a real database.
  2. A driver holds at most one offer. OFFERED is a real driver status, not a side table. A driver who is OFFERED is invisible to every other dispatch, so two trips can't wait on the same person.
  3. The state machine is a table, not a pile of ifs. ALLOWED maps each status to the statuses it may move to. Every change in the package goes through transition(), which raises before touching anything. Adding a state means adding a row, and the tests walk the whole table.
  4. Ranking and claiming are separate jobs. MatchingStrategy only sorts. It can't claim, offer or cancel. That keeps strategies trivially testable and makes a new rule (ETA-based, vehicle-type, driver-preference) a new class.
  5. Money rules are three strategies, not one. Surge, fare and cancellation change for different reasons and on different schedules. Keeping them apart means a new surge curve never risks the fare formula.
  6. Surge is locked at request time. The rider agreed to a multiplier when they asked. The trip stores it, and the final fare uses it even if surge has since dropped or spiked.
  7. Two kinds of lock, on purpose. RideService holds one RLock over the trip lifecycle, because answering an offer touches the offer, the trip and the driver together. Location pings and online/offline toggles never take it. They go through the repository's and the index's own locks. The claim CAS is what keeps those two worlds consistent.
  8. Timeouts are swept, not threaded. Each offer carries expires_at. expire_offers() sweeps the stale ones on whatever timer the deployment provides, and accept_offer re-checks the deadline itself. No background threads in the domain code, so tests drive time with ManualClock.advance().
  9. Observers can fail without consequences. EventPublisher catches Exception per observer and logs it. A dead SMS gateway must not roll back a match.

Patterns, and why each earns its place. Strategy for matching, surge, fare and cancellation, because each varies on its own. State (as a transition table) for the trip. Observer for notifications. Repository for storage, with the atomic claim on the driver repository. Facade in RideService, so a controller calls one object.

    pass


class DriverNotFoundError(NotFoundError):
    pass


class TripNotFoundError(NotFoundError):
    pass

    """The offer was never made, or it was already answered or withdrawn."""


class DuplicateIdError(RideSharingError):
    pass

    """The trip state machine refused a move, e.g. COMPLETED -> IN_PROGRESS."""

        self.current = current
        self.target = target

    """The driver holds an offer or a trip and cannot change status now."""

    """A rating was out of range, too early, or a duplicate."""

What to notice. InvalidInputError also subclasses ValueError, and NotFoundError also subclasses LookupError. from dataclasses import dataclass
from decimal import Decimal

from .errors import InvalidInputError


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class RideConfig:

    grid_cell_km: float = 1.0
    search_radius_km: float = 3.0

    default_rating: float = 5.0

    offer_timeout_s: float = 15.0
    max_offer_attempts: int = 3
    # fares

    minimum_fare: Decimal = Decimal("5.00")
    avg_speed_kmh: float = 30.0
    # surge
    surge_step: Decimal = Decimal("0.5")
    surge_cap: Decimal = Decimal("2.5")
    # cancellation
    free_cancel_window_s: float = 120.0
    rider_cancel_fee: Decimal = Decimal("4.00")

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        positive = {
            "grid_cell_km": self.grid_cell_km,
            "search_radius_km": self.search_radius_km,
            "offer_timeout_s": self.offer_timeout_s,
            "max_offer_attempts": self.max_offer_attempts,
            "avg_speed_kmh": self.avg_speed_kmh,
            "max_stars": self.max_stars,
        }
        for name, value in positive.items():
            if value <= 0:
                raise InvalidInputError(f"{name} must be positive, got {value}")
        if not 1 <= self.default_rating <= self.max_stars:
            raise InvalidInputError("default_rating must be within 1..max_stars")
        if self.surge_cap < 1:
            raise InvalidInputError("surge_cap must be at least 1")
        for name in ("base_fare", "per_km", "per_minute", "minimum_fare",
                     "rider_cancel_fee", "surge_step"):
            if getattr(self, name) < 0:
                raise InvalidInputError(f"{name} must not be negative")
        if self.free_cancel_window_s < 0 or self.rating_penalty_km_per_star < 0:
            raise InvalidInputError("windows and penalties must not be negative")

What to notice. Money fields are Decimal, built from strings. Decimal("1.20") is exact. Decimal(1.2) would carry the float's binary error into every fare. Distances and durations stay float; nobody bills by the nanometre.

Step 3 — The clock

Goal: one seam for time, so tests can expire an offer by moving the clock 16 seconds.

Type ride_sharing/clock.py:

"""The injectable clock. Offer timeouts, cancellation windows and trip
durations all read time from here, never from datetime.now() directly."""
import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from datetime import UTC, datetime, timedelta

from .errors import InvalidInputError


class Clock(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def now(self) -> datetime:
        """Current time, timezone-aware."""


class SystemClock(Clock):
    def now(self) -> datetime:
        return datetime.now(UTC)


class ManualClock(Clock):
    """A clock that moves only when a test tells it to.

    Thread-safe: the lock guards _now, because a concurrency test may read
    the time on many threads while the main thread advances it.
    """

    def __init__(self, start: datetime) -> None:
        if start.tzinfo is None:
            raise InvalidInputError("start must be timezone-aware")
        self._now = start
        self._lock = threading.Lock()

    def now(self) -> datetime:
        with self._lock:
            return self._now

    def advance(self, seconds: float) -> datetime:
        if seconds < 0:
            raise InvalidInputError("time only moves forward")
        with self._lock:
            self._now += timedelta(seconds=seconds)
            return self._now

What to notice. ManualClock has a lock even though it's test-only. The concurrency tests read the time from twenty threads at once, and a fake that races is worse than no fake. It also refuses naive datetimes. Mixing aware and naive times raises TypeError at the first comparison, far from the cause.

Step 5 — The trip state machine

Goal: write the whole lifecycle as one table and one function.

Type ride_sharing/lifecycle.py:

"""The trip state machine, as one table.

Every status change in the package goes through transition(). If an edge is
not in ALLOWED, the move is illegal and raises, with no partial update.
"""
from .errors import IllegalTransitionError
from .models import Trip, TripStatus

S = TripStatus

ALLOWED: dict[TripStatus, frozenset[TripStatus]] = {
    S.REQUESTED: frozenset({S.MATCHED, S.CANCELLED}),
    S.MATCHED: frozenset({S.DRIVER_ARRIVING, S.CANCELLED}),
    S.DRIVER_ARRIVING: frozenset({S.IN_PROGRESS, S.CANCELLED}),
    S.IN_PROGRESS: frozenset({S.COMPLETED}),
    S.COMPLETED: frozenset(),
    S.CANCELLED: frozenset(),
}


def can_transition(current: TripStatus, target: TripStatus) -> bool:
    return target in ALLOWED[current]


def transition(trip: Trip, target: TripStatus) -> None:
    """Move trip to target, or raise IllegalTransitionError and change nothing."""
    if not can_transition(trip.status, target):
        raise IllegalTransitionError(trip.trip_id, trip.status, target)
    trip.status = target

What to notice. Read ALLOWED top to bottom and you've read the business rules. IN_PROGRESS has no CANCELLED edge: once the rider is in the car, the trip ends by completing. COMPLETED and CANCELLED map to empty sets, so terminal really means terminal. The check runs before the assignment, so a refused move changes nothing.

Step 6 — The spatial index

Goal: answer "which drivers are within 3 km?" without looking at every driver in the city.

Type ride_sharing/spatial.py:

"""Find drivers near a point without scanning every driver in the city.

GridIndex buckets drivers into square cells. A radius query visits only the
cells that the search circle can touch, then checks exact distances.
"""
import math
import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod

from .errors import InvalidInputError
from .models import Location

Cell = tuple[int, int]


class SpatialIndex(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def upsert(self, driver_id: str, location: Location) -> None:
        """Insert the driver, or move them if already indexed."""

    @abstractmethod
    def remove(self, driver_id: str) -> None:
        """Forget the driver. Unknown ids are ignored."""

    @abstractmethod
    def within(self, center: Location, radius_km: float) -> list[tuple[str, float]]:
        """(driver_id, distance_km) pairs inside the radius, nearest first."""


class GridIndex(SpatialIndex):
    """Uniform grid. One lock guards both maps, so a driver is never in two
    cells at once even while location pings and searches run in parallel."""

    def __init__(self, cell_km: float) -> None:
        if cell_km <= 0:
            raise InvalidInputError("cell_km must be positive")
        self._cell_km = cell_km
        self._cells: dict[Cell, set[str]] = {}
        self._where: dict[str, tuple[Cell, Location]] = {}
        self._lock = threading.Lock()

    def cell_of(self, location: Location) -> Cell:
        return (math.floor(location.x_km / self._cell_km),
                math.floor(location.y_km / self._cell_km))

    def upsert(self, driver_id: str, location: Location) -> None:
        cell = self.cell_of(location)
        with self._lock:
            self._detach(driver_id)
            self._cells.setdefault(cell, set()).add(driver_id)
            self._where[driver_id] = (cell, location)

    def remove(self, driver_id: str) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._detach(driver_id)

    def within(self, center: Location, radius_km: float) -> list[tuple[str, float]]:
        if radius_km < 0:
            raise InvalidInputError("radius_km must not be negative")
        cx, cy = self.cell_of(center)
        span = math.ceil(radius_km / self._cell_km)
        found: list[tuple[str, float]] = []
        with self._lock:
            for gx in range(cx - span, cx + span + 1):
                for gy in range(cy - span, cy + span + 1):
                    for driver_id in self._cells.get((gx, gy), ()):
                        distance = center.distance_to(self._where[driver_id][1])
                        if distance <= radius_km:
                            found.append((driver_id, distance))
        found.sort(key=lambda pair: (pair[1], pair[0]))
        return found

    def _detach(self, driver_id: str) -> None:
        """Caller holds self._lock."""
        entry = self._where.pop(driver_id, None)
        if entry is None:
            return
        bucket = self._cells[entry[0]]
        bucket.discard(driver_id)
        if not bucket:
            del self._cells[entry[0]]

What to notice. math.floor, not int(). int(-0.2) is 0, which would put a driver just west of the origin in the same cell as one just east of it, and the grid would silently fold in half. The radius query visits a square of (2·span + 1)² cells, then filters by exact distance, because a square of cells covers more than the circle. Sorting by (distance, driver_id) keeps results deterministic when two drivers sit at the same distance.

Goal: type the storage interfaces and their in-memory versions, including claim, the compare-and-set this chapter rests on.

Type ride_sharing/repositories.py:

"""Storage behind interfaces: riders, drivers, trips.

The driver repository owns one extra operation, claim(), an atomic
compare-and-set on driver status. It is the line that stops a driver from
ever being handed two riders.
"""
import threading
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod

from .errors import DriverNotFoundError, DuplicateIdError, RiderNotFoundError, TripNotFoundError
from .models import Driver, DriverStatus, Location, Rider, Trip, TripStatus


class RiderRepository(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def add(self, rider: Rider) -> None: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def get(self, rider_id: str) -> Rider: ...


class DriverRepository(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def add(self, driver: Driver) -> None: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def get(self, driver_id: str) -> Driver: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def claim(self, driver_id: str, expected: DriverStatus, new: DriverStatus) -> bool:
        """Set status to new only if it is currently expected. Atomic."""

    @abstractmethod
    def move(self, driver_id: str, location: Location) -> None: ...


class TripRepository(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def add(self, trip: Trip) -> None: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def get(self, trip_id: str) -> Trip: ...

    @abstractmethod
    def with_status(self, status: TripStatus) -> list[Trip]: ...


class InMemoryRiders(RiderRepository):
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._riders: dict[str, Rider] = {}
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _riders

    def add(self, rider: Rider) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            if rider.rider_id in self._riders:
                raise DuplicateIdError(f"rider {rider.rider_id} already exists")
            self._riders[rider.rider_id] = rider

    def get(self, rider_id: str) -> Rider:
        with self._lock:
            try:
                return self._riders[rider_id]
            except KeyError:
                raise RiderNotFoundError(f"no rider {rider_id}") from None


class InMemoryDrivers(DriverRepository):
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._drivers: dict[str, Driver] = {}
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _drivers, Driver.status, Driver.location

    def add(self, driver: Driver) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            if driver.driver_id in self._drivers:
                raise DuplicateIdError(f"driver {driver.driver_id} already exists")
            self._drivers[driver.driver_id] = driver

    def get(self, driver_id: str) -> Driver:
        with self._lock:
            try:
                return self._drivers[driver_id]
            except KeyError:
                raise DriverNotFoundError(f"no driver {driver_id}") from None

    def claim(self, driver_id: str, expected: DriverStatus, new: DriverStatus) -> bool:
        with self._lock:
            driver = self._drivers.get(driver_id)
            if driver is None:
                raise DriverNotFoundError(f"no driver {driver_id}")
            if driver.status is not expected:
                return False
            driver.status = new
            return True

    def move(self, driver_id: str, location: Location) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            driver = self._drivers.get(driver_id)
            if driver is None:
                raise DriverNotFoundError(f"no driver {driver_id}")
            driver.location = location


class InMemoryTrips(TripRepository):
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._trips: dict[str, Trip] = {}
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _trips (not the trips' fields)

    def add(self, trip: Trip) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            if trip.trip_id in self._trips:
                raise DuplicateIdError(f"trip {trip.trip_id} already exists")
            self._trips[trip.trip_id] = trip

    def get(self, trip_id: str) -> Trip:
        with self._lock:
            try:
                return self._trips[trip_id]
            except KeyError:
                raise TripNotFoundError(f"no trip {trip_id}") from None

    def with_status(self, status: TripStatus) -> list[Trip]:
        with self._lock:
            return [t for t in self._trips.values() if t.status is status]

What to notice. claim returns False instead of raising when the status doesn't match. Losing a race is normal during dispatch, not an error. The caller moves on to the next driver. An unknown id, on the other hand, is a bug and raises. Also note the comment on each lock: it says exactly what the lock guards.

Step 8 — Matching strategies

Goal: two ways to rank the same candidates.

Type ride_sharing/matching.py:

"""Matching strategies: given a pickup and some free drivers, who goes first?

A strategy only ranks. It never claims a driver or touches a trip, so a new
ranking rule is a new class and nothing else changes.
"""
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from collections.abc import Sequence

from .config import RideConfig
from .models import Driver, Location


class MatchingStrategy(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def rank(self, pickup: Location, candidates: Sequence[Driver]) -> list[Driver]:
        """Best candidate first. Must be deterministic for equal inputs."""


class NearestDriverStrategy(MatchingStrategy):
    def rank(self, pickup: Location, candidates: Sequence[Driver]) -> list[Driver]:
        return sorted(candidates,
                      key=lambda d: (pickup.distance_to(d.location), d.driver_id))


class RatingWeightedStrategy(MatchingStrategy):
    """Score = distance + penalty per star below the maximum. Lowest wins.

    With the default 0.5 km per star, a 5.0 driver 1.4 km away beats a 4.0
    driver 1.0 km away (1.4 < 1.0 + 0.5).
    """

    def __init__(self, config: RideConfig) -> None:
        self._config = config

    def score(self, pickup: Location, driver: Driver) -> float:
        rating = driver.ratings.average(self._config.default_rating)
        missing_stars = self._config.max_stars - rating
        return (pickup.distance_to(driver.location)
                + self._config.rating_penalty_km_per_star * missing_stars)

    def rank(self, pickup: Location, candidates: Sequence[Driver]) -> list[Driver]:
        return sorted(candidates, key=lambda d: (self.score(pickup, d), d.driver_id))

What to notice. The rating weight is measured in kilometres. "Half a kilometre per missing star" is something a product manager can reason about. An abstract weight like 0.37 isn't. A new driver with no ratings gets default_rating, so they aren't punished for being new.

Step 9 — Surge, fares and cancellation fees

Goal: three small money strategies, each reading RideConfig.

Type ride_sharing/pricing.py:

"""Money rules, each behind its own interface.

SurgePolicy turns supply and demand into a multiplier. FareStrategy turns a
distance, a duration and that multiplier into a fare. CancellationPolicy
decides whether a cancelled trip costs anything. All three read RideConfig.
"""
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from datetime import datetime
from decimal import ROUND_HALF_UP, Decimal

from .config import RideConfig
from .errors import InvalidInputError
from .models import CancelledBy, Trip

CENT = Decimal("0.01")
TENTH = Decimal("0.1")
ONE = Decimal("1.0")


def to_money(value: Decimal) -> Decimal:
    return value.quantize(CENT, rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP)


class SurgePolicy(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def multiplier(self, demand: int, supply: int) -> Decimal:
        """demand = open requests near the pickup, supply = free drivers there."""


class SupplyDemandSurge(SurgePolicy):
    """1.0x while drivers keep up; then +surge_step per extra request per
    driver, rounded to 0.1 and capped. No drivers at all means the cap."""

    def __init__(self, config: RideConfig) -> None:
        self._config = config

    def multiplier(self, demand: int, supply: int) -> Decimal:
        if demand < 0 or supply < 0:
            raise InvalidInputError("demand and supply must not be negative")
        if demand <= supply:
            return ONE
        if supply == 0:
            return self._config.surge_cap
        ratio = Decimal(demand) / Decimal(supply)
        raw = ONE + self._config.surge_step * (ratio - ONE)
        return min(self._config.surge_cap, raw.quantize(TENTH, rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP))


class FareStrategy(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def fare(self, distance_km: float, minutes: float, surge: Decimal) -> Decimal: ...


class StandardFare(FareStrategy):
    """max(base + per_km*km + per_minute*min, minimum) * surge, to the cent."""

    def __init__(self, config: RideConfig) -> None:
        self._config = config

    def fare(self, distance_km: float, minutes: float, surge: Decimal) -> Decimal:
        if distance_km < 0 or minutes < 0:
            raise InvalidInputError("distance and minutes must not be negative")
        c = self._config
        subtotal = (c.base_fare
                    + c.per_km * Decimal(str(round(distance_km, 3)))
                    + c.per_minute * Decimal(str(round(minutes, 2))))
        return to_money(max(subtotal, c.minimum_fare) * surge)


class CancellationPolicy(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def fee(self, trip: Trip, by: CancelledBy, now: datetime) -> Decimal: ...


class GracePeriodCancellation(CancellationPolicy):
    """Riders cancel free until free_cancel_window_s after a driver accepts.
    After that they pay rider_cancel_fee. Drivers and the system never
    charge the rider."""

    def __init__(self, config: RideConfig) -> None:
        self._config = config

    def fee(self, trip: Trip, by: CancelledBy, now: datetime) -> Decimal:
        if by is not CancelledBy.RIDER or trip.matched_at is None:
            return to_money(Decimal(0))
        waited = (now - trip.matched_at).total_seconds()
        if waited <= self._config.free_cancel_window_s:
            return to_money(Decimal(0))
        return to_money(self._config.rider_cancel_fee)

What to notice. The surge curve is linear in the demand-to-supply ratio, rounded to a tenth, and capped. Zero supply jumps straight to the cap rather than dividing by zero. The minimum fare applies before surge, so a short hop during a 2x surge costs twice the minimum. That's a product decision. It's written down in one docstring, so it can be argued with.

import logging
import threading

from dataclasses import dataclass
from datetime import datetime
from enum import Enum

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)


class EventKind(Enum):
    REQUESTED = "REQUESTED"
    OFFERED = "OFFERED"
    OFFER_DECLINED = "OFFER_DECLINED"
    OFFER_EXPIRED = "OFFER_EXPIRED"
    MATCHED = "MATCHED"
    DRIVER_ARRIVING = "DRIVER_ARRIVING"
    STARTED = "STARTED"
    COMPLETED = "COMPLETED"
    CANCELLED = "CANCELLED"


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class TripEvent:
    kind: EventKind
    trip_id: str
    at: datetime
    driver_id: str | None = None
    detail: str = ""


class TripObserver(ABC):
    @abstractmethod
    def notify(self, event: TripEvent) -> None: ...


class EventPublisher:
    """Fans each event out to every subscriber.

    A failing observer is logged and skipped: a broken SMS gateway must never
    roll back a match. The lock guards the subscriber list only.
    """

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._observers: list[TripObserver] = []
        self._lock = threading.Lock()

    def subscribe(self, observer: TripObserver) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self._observers.append(observer)

    def publish(self, event: TripEvent) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            observers = list(self._observers)
        for observer in observers:
            try:
                observer.notify(event)
            except Exception:  # isolate observers; never a bare except
                log.exception("observer %r failed on %s", observer, event.kind.value)


class RecordingObserver(TripObserver):
    """Keeps every event in order. Used by tests; handy in demos."""

    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self.events: list[TripEvent] = []
        self._lock = threading.Lock()

    def notify(self, event: TripEvent) -> None:
        with self._lock:
            self.events.append(event)

    def kinds(self, trip_id: str | None = None) -> list[EventKind]:
        with self._lock:
            return [e.kind for e in self.events
                    if trip_id is None or e.trip_id == trip_id]

What to notice. publish copies the subscriber list under the lock, then calls observers outside it. A slow observer can't block a new subscription. And except Exception here is deliberate and narrow in purpose: isolate one observer from the others, and log the traceback with log.exception. It's not a bare except, so KeyboardInterrupt still gets through.

Step 11 — The dispatcher

Goal: find, rank, claim, offer. Keep the table of pending offers.

Type ride_sharing/dispatch.py:

"""Dispatch: find free drivers near a pickup, claim the best one, offer.

The dispatcher owns the pending-offer table. It asks the spatial index who
is near, the matching strategy who is best, and the driver repository to
claim that driver atomically. If the claim loses a race (the driver just
went offline, or another trip got them), it simply tries the next driver.
"""
import itertools
import logging
import threading
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

from .clock import Clock
from .config import RideConfig
from .errors import OfferNotFoundError
from .matching import MatchingStrategy
from .models import Driver, DriverStatus, Location, Offer, Trip
from .repositories import DriverRepository
from .spatial import SpatialIndex

log = logging.getLogger(__name__)


class Dispatcher:
    def __init__(self, drivers: DriverRepository, index: SpatialIndex,
                 strategy: MatchingStrategy, clock: Clock, config: RideConfig) -> None:
        self._drivers = drivers
        self._index = index
        self._strategy = strategy
        self._clock = clock
        self._config = config
        self._offers: dict[str, Offer] = {}
        self._offer_by_trip: dict[str, str] = {}
        self._seq = itertools.count(1)
        self._lock = threading.Lock()  # guards _offers, _offer_by_trip, _seq

    def available_near(self, location: Location) -> list[Driver]:
        nearby = self._index.within(location, self._config.search_radius_km)
        drivers = (self._drivers.get(driver_id) for driver_id, _ in nearby)
        return [d for d in drivers if d.status is DriverStatus.AVAILABLE]

    def offer_next(self, trip: Trip) -> Offer | None:
        """Claim the best untried driver and offer them the trip, or None."""
        if trip.offer_attempts >= self._config.max_offer_attempts:
            return None
        candidates = [d for d in self.available_near(trip.pickup)
                      if d.driver_id not in trip.declined_driver_ids]
        for driver in self._strategy.rank(trip.pickup, candidates):
            if not self._drivers.claim(driver.driver_id,
                                       DriverStatus.AVAILABLE, DriverStatus.OFFERED):
                log.debug("lost claim on %s; trying next", driver.driver_id)
                continue
            offer = self._record(trip.trip_id, driver.driver_id, self._clock.now())
            trip.offer_attempts += 1
            log.info("offered %s to %s (attempt %d)", trip.trip_id,
                     driver.driver_id, trip.offer_attempts)
            return offer
        return None

    def take(self, offer_id: str) -> Offer:
        """Remove and return a pending offer. Each offer can be taken once."""
        with self._lock:
            offer = self._offers.pop(offer_id, None)
            if offer is None:
                raise OfferNotFoundError(f"no pending offer {offer_id}")
            del self._offer_by_trip[offer.trip_id]
            return offer

    def withdraw(self, trip_id: str) -> Offer | None:
        with self._lock:
            offer_id = self._offer_by_trip.pop(trip_id, None)
            return None if offer_id is None else self._offers.pop(offer_id)

    def pending_for(self, trip_id: str) -> Offer | None:
        with self._lock:
            offer_id = self._offer_by_trip.get(trip_id)
            return None if offer_id is None else self._offers[offer_id]

    def expired(self, now: datetime) -> list[Offer]:
        with self._lock:
            return sorted((o for o in self._offers.values() if o.is_expired(now)),
                          key=lambda o: (o.expires_at, o.offer_id))

    def _record(self, trip_id: str, driver_id: str, now: datetime) -> Offer:
        with self._lock:
            offer = Offer(offer_id=f"O{next(self._seq)}", trip_id=trip_id,
                          driver_id=driver_id,
                          expires_at=now + timedelta(seconds=self._config.offer_timeout_s))
            self._offers[offer.offer_id] = offer
            self._offer_by_trip[trip_id] = offer.offer_id
            return offer

What to notice. Look at the loop in offer_next. The strategy ranks a snapshot, and the snapshot may already be stale. That's fine, because nothing is promised until claim succeeds. If the claim fails, the loop just continues. This is optimistic concurrency, and it's why ranking needs no lock at all. take pops the offer, so an offer can be answered exactly once, whoever gets there first.

Type ride_sharing/service.py:

picks drivers, the state table judges transitions, the pricing classes do

def _require_text(value: str, name: str) -> str:
    if not isinstance(value, str) or not value.strip():
        raise InvalidInputError(f"{name} must be a non-blank string")
    return value.strip()


class RideService:
    def __init__(
        self,
        *,
        config: RideConfig | None = None,
        clock: Clock | None = None,
        riders: RiderRepository | None = None,
        drivers: DriverRepository | None = None,
        trips: TripRepository | None = None,
        index: SpatialIndex | None = None,
        matching: MatchingStrategy | None = None,
        surge: SurgePolicy | None = None,
        fares: FareStrategy | None = None,
        cancellation: CancellationPolicy | None = None,
        events: EventPublisher | None = None,
    ) -> None:
        self._config = config or RideConfig()
        self._clock = clock or SystemClock()
        self._riders = riders or InMemoryRiders()
        self._drivers = drivers or InMemoryDrivers()
        self._trips = trips or InMemoryTrips()
        self._index = index or GridIndex(self._config.grid_cell_km)
        self._surge = surge or SupplyDemandSurge(self._config)
        self._fares = fares or StandardFare(self._config)
        self._cancellation = cancellation or GracePeriodCancellation(self._config)
        self._events = events or EventPublisher()
        self._dispatcher = Dispatcher(self._drivers, self._index,
                                      matching or NearestDriverStrategy(),
                                      self._clock, self._config)
        self._trip_seq = itertools.count(1)
        # Guards every Trip field, the trip id sequence, and the
        # answer-an-offer sequence (take offer -> move trip -> move driver).
        self._lock = threading.RLock()

    def subscribe(self, observer: TripObserver) -> None:
        self._events.subscribe(observer)

    # -- people ---------------------------------------------------------

    def register_rider(self, rider_id: str, name: str) -> Rider:
        rider = Rider(_require_text(rider_id, "rider_id"), _require_text(name, "name"))
        self._riders.add(rider)
        return rider

    def register_driver(self, driver_id: str, name: str, location: Location) -> Driver:
        driver = Driver(_require_text(driver_id, "driver_id"),
                        _require_text(name, "name"), location)
        self._drivers.add(driver)
        return driver

    def get_driver(self, driver_id: str) -> Driver:
        return self._drivers.get(driver_id)

    def go_online(self, driver_id: str) -> None:
        driver = self._drivers.get(driver_id)
        if driver.status is DriverStatus.AVAILABLE:
            return
        if not self._drivers.claim(driver_id, DriverStatus.OFFLINE, DriverStatus.AVAILABLE):
            raise DriverBusyError(f"{driver_id} is {driver.status.value}")
        self._index.upsert(driver_id, driver.location)
        log.info("%s online at %s", driver_id, driver.location)

    def go_offline(self, driver_id: str) -> None:
        driver = self._drivers.get(driver_id)
        if driver.status is DriverStatus.OFFLINE:
            return
        if not self._drivers.claim(driver_id, DriverStatus.AVAILABLE, DriverStatus.OFFLINE):
            raise DriverBusyError(f"{driver_id} is {driver.status.value}")
        self._index.remove(driver_id)
        log.info("%s offline", driver_id)

    def update_location(self, driver_id: str, location: Location) -> None:
        """The hot path: called every few seconds per driver. No service lock."""
        self._drivers.move(driver_id, location)
        if self._drivers.get(driver_id).status is not DriverStatus.OFFLINE:
            self._index.upsert(driver_id, location)

    # -- pricing --------------------------------------------------------

    def quote(self, pickup: Location, dropoff: Location) -> FareQuote:
        with self._lock:
            surge = self._current_surge(pickup)
        distance = pickup.distance_to(dropoff)
        minutes = distance / self._config.avg_speed_kmh * 60
        return FareQuote(round(distance, 3), round(minutes, 2), surge,
                         self._fares.fare(distance, minutes, surge))

    # -- the ride -------------------------------------------------------

    def request_ride(self, rider_id: str, pickup: Location, dropoff: Location) -> Trip:
        self._riders.get(rider_id)  # unknown rider fails before anything changes
        if pickup == dropoff:
            raise InvalidInputError("pickup and dropoff must differ")
        with self._lock:
            now = self._clock.now()
            trip = Trip(trip_id=f"T{next(self._trip_seq)}", rider_id=rider_id,
                        pickup=pickup, dropoff=dropoff, requested_at=now,
                        surge=self._current_surge(pickup))
            self._trips.add(trip)
            self._publish(EventKind.REQUESTED, trip, detail=f"surge {trip.surge}x")
            self._offer_or_cancel(trip)
            return trip

    def accept_offer(self, offer_id: str) -> Trip:
        with self._lock:
            offer = self._dispatcher.take(offer_id)
            trip = self._trips.get(offer.trip_id)
            if offer.is_expired(self._clock.now()):
                self._release_and_reoffer(offer, trip, EventKind.OFFER_EXPIRED)
                raise OfferExpiredError(f"offer {offer_id} expired at {offer.expires_at}")
            transition(trip, TripStatus.MATCHED)
            self._must_claim(offer.driver_id, DriverStatus.OFFERED, DriverStatus.ON_TRIP)
            trip.driver_id = offer.driver_id
            trip.matched_at = self._clock.now()
            self._publish(EventKind.MATCHED, trip)
            return trip

    def decline_offer(self, offer_id: str) -> Trip:
        with self._lock:
            offer = self._dispatcher.take(offer_id)
            trip = self._trips.get(offer.trip_id)
            self._release_and_reoffer(offer, trip, EventKind.OFFER_DECLINED)
            return trip

    def expire_offers(self) -> list[Trip]:
        """Sweep offers past their deadline; call it from a timer."""
        with self._lock:
            touched = []
            for stale in self._dispatcher.expired(self._clock.now()):
                offer = self._dispatcher.take(stale.offer_id)
                trip = self._trips.get(offer.trip_id)
                self._release_and_reoffer(offer, trip, EventKind.OFFER_EXPIRED)
                touched.append(trip)
            return touched

    def pending_offer(self, trip_id: str) -> Offer | None:
        return self._dispatcher.pending_for(trip_id)

    def driver_arriving(self, trip_id: str) -> Trip:
        with self._lock:
            trip = self._trips.get(trip_id)
            transition(trip, TripStatus.DRIVER_ARRIVING)
            self._publish(EventKind.DRIVER_ARRIVING, trip)
            return trip

    def start_trip(self, trip_id: str) -> Trip:
        with self._lock:
            trip = self._trips.get(trip_id)
            transition(trip, TripStatus.IN_PROGRESS)
            trip.started_at = self._clock.now()
            self._publish(EventKind.STARTED, trip)
            return trip

    def complete_trip(self, trip_id: str) -> Trip:
        with self._lock:
            trip = self._trips.get(trip_id)
            transition(trip, TripStatus.COMPLETED)
            now = self._clock.now()
            trip.completed_at = now
            minutes = (now - trip.started_at).total_seconds() / 60
            trip.fare = self._fares.fare(trip.distance_km, minutes, trip.surge)
            self._drivers.move(trip.driver_id, trip.dropoff)
            self._index.upsert(trip.driver_id, trip.dropoff)
            self._must_claim(trip.driver_id, DriverStatus.ON_TRIP, DriverStatus.AVAILABLE)
            self._publish(EventKind.COMPLETED, trip, detail=f"fare ${trip.fare}")
            return trip

    def cancel_trip(self, trip_id: str, by: CancelledBy, reason: str = "") -> Trip:
        if not isinstance(by, CancelledBy):
            raise InvalidInputError("by must be a CancelledBy")
        with self._lock:
            trip = self._trips.get(trip_id)
            self._cancel(trip, by, reason or f"cancelled by {by.value.lower()}")
            return trip

    def get_trip(self, trip_id: str) -> Trip:
        return self._trips.get(trip_id)

    # -- ratings --------------------------------------------------------

    def rate_driver(self, trip_id: str, stars: int) -> float:
        """The rider rates the driver. Returns the driver's new average."""
        with self._lock:
            trip = self._rateable(trip_id, stars, already=lambda t: t.driver_rated)
            driver = self._drivers.get(trip.driver_id)
            driver.ratings.add(stars)
            trip.driver_rated = True
            return driver.ratings.average(self._config.default_rating)

    def rate_rider(self, trip_id: str, stars: int) -> float:
        """The driver rates the rider. Returns the rider's new average."""
        with self._lock:
            trip = self._rateable(trip_id, stars, already=lambda t: t.rider_rated)
            rider = self._riders.get(trip.rider_id)
            rider.ratings.add(stars)
            trip.rider_rated = True
            return rider.ratings.average(self._config.default_rating)

    # -- internals (caller holds self._lock) -----------------------------

    def _current_surge(self, pickup: Location) -> Decimal:
        radius = self._config.search_radius_km
        waiting = sum(1 for t in self._trips.with_status(TripStatus.REQUESTED)
                      if t.pickup.distance_to(pickup) <= radius)
        supply = len(self._dispatcher.available_near(pickup))
        return self._surge.multiplier(demand=waiting + 1, supply=supply)

    def _offer_or_cancel(self, trip: Trip) -> None:
        offer = self._dispatcher.offer_next(trip)
        if offer is None:
            self._cancel(trip, CancelledBy.SYSTEM, NO_DRIVER)
        else:
            self._publish(EventKind.OFFERED, trip, driver_id=offer.driver_id,
                          detail=f"offer {offer.offer_id}")

    def _release_and_reoffer(self, offer: Offer, trip: Trip, kind: EventKind) -> None:
        self._must_claim(offer.driver_id, DriverStatus.OFFERED, DriverStatus.AVAILABLE)
        trip.declined_driver_ids.add(offer.driver_id)
        self._publish(kind, trip, driver_id=offer.driver_id)
        self._offer_or_cancel(trip)

    def _cancel(self, trip: Trip, by: CancelledBy, reason: str) -> None:
        transition(trip, TripStatus.CANCELLED)  # raises first, before any change
        now = self._clock.now()
        trip.cancellation_fee = self._cancellation.fee(trip, by, now)
        trip.cancelled_at, trip.cancelled_by, trip.cancel_reason = now, by, reason
        pending = self._dispatcher.withdraw(trip.trip_id)
        if pending is not None:
            self._must_claim(pending.driver_id, DriverStatus.OFFERED, DriverStatus.AVAILABLE)
        if trip.driver_id is not None:
            self._must_claim(trip.driver_id, DriverStatus.ON_TRIP, DriverStatus.AVAILABLE)
        self._publish(EventKind.CANCELLED, trip, detail=f"{reason}; fee ${trip.cancellation_fee}")

    def _rateable(self, trip_id: str, stars: int,
                  already: Callable[[Trip], bool]) -> Trip:
        if isinstance(stars, bool) or not isinstance(stars, int) \
                or not 1 <= stars <= self._config.max_stars:
            raise RatingError(f"stars must be an int in 1..{self._config.max_stars}")
        trip = self._trips.get(trip_id)
        if trip.status is not TripStatus.COMPLETED:
            raise RatingError(f"trip {trip_id} is {trip.status}, not COMPLETED")
        if already(trip):
            raise RatingError(f"trip {trip_id} already rated")
        return trip

    def _must_claim(self, driver_id: str, expected: DriverStatus, new: DriverStatus) -> None:
        if not self._drivers.claim(driver_id, expected, new):
            raise DriverBusyError(f"{driver_id} was not {expected.value}")

    def _publish(self, kind: EventKind, trip: Trip, *, driver_id: str | None = None,
                 detail: str = "") -> None:
        at: datetime = self._clock.now()
        event = TripEvent(kind, trip.trip_id, at, driver_id or trip.driver_id, detail)
        log.info("%s %s %s", trip.trip_id, kind.value, detail)
        self._events.publish(event)

What to notice. Three things.

First, _cancel calls transition before it touches anything else, so an illegal cancel (say, mid-trip) leaves the trip, the driver and the offers exactly as they were.

Second, _release_and_reoffer is shared by decline, timeout and late accept. Three triggers, one code path, so they can't drift apart.

Third, update_location takes no service lock. A city's worth of drivers pinging every few seconds would otherwise queue behind dispatch. It's safe because ranking always re-checks status and the claim is atomic.

class ConcurrencyTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_concurrent_requests_never_double_assign_a_driver(self):
        service, _, _ = make_service()
        for i in range(4):
            add_driver(service, f"d{i}", 0.1 * i, 0.0)
        for i in range(2, 21):
            service.register_rider(f"r{i}", f"Rider {i}")
        riders = ["r1"] + [f"r{i}" for i in range(2, 21)]
        barrier = threading.Barrier(len(riders))
        trips = []

        def ride(rider_id):
            barrier.wait()
            trips.append(service.request_ride(rider_id, HOME, OFFICE))

        threads = [threading.Thread(target=ride, args=(r,)) for r in riders]
        for t in threads:
            t.start()
        for t in threads:
            t.join()
        offered = [offer_of(service, t).driver_id for t in trips
                   if t.status is TripStatus.REQUESTED]
        self.assertEqual(sorted(offered), ["d0", "d1", "d2", "d3"])
        self.assertEqual(sum(t.status is TripStatus.CANCELLED for t in trips), 16)

    def test_same_offer_accepted_twice_has_one_winner(self):
        service, _, _ = make_service()
        add_driver(service, "d1", 0.5, 0.0)
        trip = service.request_ride("r1", HOME, OFFICE)
        offer_id = offer_of(service, trip).offer_id
        barrier = threading.Barrier(2)
        outcomes = []

        def accept():
            barrier.wait()
            try:
                service.accept_offer(offer_id)
                outcomes.append("won")
            except OfferNotFoundError:
                outcomes.append("lost")

        threads = [threading.Thread(target=accept) for _ in range(2)]
        for t in threads:
            t.start()
        for t in threads:
            t.join()
        self.assertEqual(sorted(outcomes), ["lost", "won"])
        self.assertIs(trip.status, TripStatus.MATCHED)

    def test_claim_is_atomic_under_contention(self):
        drivers = InMemoryDrivers()
        drivers.add(Driver("d1", "D1", HOME, status=DriverStatus.AVAILABLE))
        barrier = threading.Barrier(16)
        wins = []

        def grab():
            barrier.wait()
            if drivers.claim("d1", DriverStatus.AVAILABLE, DriverStatus.OFFERED):
                wins.append(1)

        threads = [threading.Thread(target=grab) for _ in range(16)]
        for t in threads:
            t.start()
        for t in threads:
            t.join()
        self.assertEqual(len(wins), 1)

Honest limits. The locks cover threads in one process. Two dispatcher processes would need the claim to move into shared storage (see the follow-ups). One service lock serializes the whole city's lifecycle. That's fine for an interview and wrong for production, where you'd shard by region. Observers run synchronously while the lock is held, so a slow observer slows dispatch; a real system writes events to an outbox and delivers them asynchronously. Distances are straight lines on a flat grid, not road ETAs. Nothing checks that the driver calling start_trip is the assigned one.

9. Common interview follow-ups

"Now run three dispatcher instances behind a load balancer." The in-process lock no longer protects anything. Move the claim into shared storage: UPDATE drivers SET status='OFFERED', offer_id=? WHERE id=? AND status='AVAILABLE' and check the row count, or a Redis SET driver:{id}:lock <offer> NX PX 15000 whose TTL doubles as the offer timeout. Then shard dispatch by region (city, or geohash prefix) so each region has one active dispatcher and the service lock becomes a per-region lock. DriverRepository.claim is already the seam; only its implementation changes.

"Use real latitude and longitude, and a geohash instead of your grid." Write GeohashIndex(SpatialIndex): encode each position to a precision-6 geohash (about 1.2 km by 0.6 km cells), store drivers by hash, and search the cell plus its 8 neighbours. Location gains lat and lng and a haversine distance_to. Dispatch, matching and pricing don't change, because they only ever see within() and distance_to(). In production you'd likely reach for Redis GEOSEARCH or Uber's H3 hexagons behind the same interface.

"A driver cancels after accepting. Don't cancel the trip; find another driver." Add MATCHED → REQUESTED and DRIVER_ARRIVING → REQUESTED to ALLOWED. In cancel_trip with CancelledBy.DRIVER, release the driver, add them to declined_driver_ids, clear driver_id and matched_at, and call _offer_or_cancel. The lifecycle tests then fail until you update them, which is exactly what you want from a table-driven state machine.

"Add shared rides (pool)." This is the biggest move. A driver gets a seat capacity, and ON_TRIP stops being exclusive. Claiming becomes "decrement free seats if enough remain", still a compare-and-set. Matching changes from distance-to-pickup to detour cost on the driver's current route, so it needs a new MatchingStrategy that sees the active trips. The fare strategy gets a pool discount. The per-trip state machine stays as it is; each rider still has their own trip.

"Greedy first-come matching is unfair at rush hour. Improve it." Switch to batch matching. Collect requests for a short window (say two seconds), build a cost matrix of riders by drivers, and solve the assignment problem (Hungarian algorithm, or a greedy approximation for big batches). That's a new BatchDispatcher beside Dispatcher. The claim CAS and the state machine are untouched.

"Why offer to one driver at a time instead of broadcasting?" Broadcasting makes acceptance faster but turns every accept into a race that most drivers lose, which they hate. Sequential offers keep the "one driver, one offer" invariant simple. If the business wants broadcast, offer to the top three and let the first accept_offer win: Dispatcher.take already guarantees exactly one winner, and the losers get OfferNotFoundError.

"Where does the 15-second timeout actually fire?" Not in a sleeping thread per offer. A scheduler calls expire_offers() every second, or each offer goes on a delay queue keyed by expires_at. accept_offer re-checks the deadline anyway, so a sweeper running late can never let a stale accept through.

"Why lock surge at request time?" Because the rider agreed to it. Recomputing at drop-off would let the price change under them, which is both a trust problem and, in several markets, a regulatory one. The trip stores surge, and complete_trip uses it.
